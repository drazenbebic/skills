# Game Stories Turn Generic Without Owned Specifics

A game story stops feeling generic when four things hold at once, and the craft literature supplies a checkable test for each. First, its beats are joined by cause ("but" and "therefore", never "and then") and every unit turns a value, which is the best-sourced rule in story construction, running from Aristotle through McKee to Story Grid. Second, its theme is carried by what the player does, as in Papers, Please, Undertale, Outer Wilds and Hades, rather than by cutscenes that most players will never reach: trophy and achievement data show that even well-loved long games are finished by a minority. Third, its particulars (places, customs, jobs, creatures, losses) trace to the owner's own life, a closely observed procedure, or named research and consultants, never to genre defaults. Fourth, it lives in a status-marked canon document whose beats carry stable IDs, world-state preconditions and postconditions that dialogue, quest design, voice and localisation can all cite. These are precisely the choices language models get wrong by default. Across peer-reviewed studies and 2026 preprints, model-written stories front-load their climaxes, resolve tension almost completely, write friendly casts and community-festival endings, and converge on the same names and settings across vendors, while rubric-based model judges rate those same stories above New Yorker fiction. The countermeasures that have evidence act on structure and process rather than wording: explicit turning points in the outline, a reference story's beat structure adapted to the new idea, divergent generation with deduplication, post-generation scans instead of banned-word prompts, and blind, checklist-driven audits. For an existing game the professional sequence is to extract the as-built story from the build and its data before judging it, to test each unit for a value turn, a crisis and a protagonist decision, and to prefer additive revision, because the documented late tear-downs (Destiny, Anthem) shipped incoherent stories. Most of this guidance is practitioner heuristic rather than measurement. No study has tested LLM story failures on game narratives, and no public standard fixes pacing numbers, so a skill built on this report should expose those numbers as parameters and leave verdicts on originality, tone and authenticity to the owner, to playtests and to consultants.

## Fifteen terms the skill will use

The story layer sits above the line layer covered in [game-dialogue.md](game-dialogue.md), whose vocabulary (bark, state and flag, storylet, branch-and-bottleneck, ludonarrative dissonance) applies here unchanged. The terms below are the ones this report adds. Each is sourced where it is first used in the body.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Beat | The smallest unit of story change. In a game it is a change in world state that the player does, finds, chooses or watches |
| Value turn | McKee's test for a scene: a value-charged condition (alive/dead, trusted/betrayed, free/captive) changes charge between entry and exit |
| Fabula and syuzhet | The true chronological order of events versus the order in which the audience learns them |
| Controlling idea | McKee's one-sentence theme: the value that wins at the climax plus the cause that makes it win |
| Want, need and Lie | The conscious external goal, the inner truth the character lacks, and the false belief that blocks it (Weiland) |
| Flat arc | The protagonist already holds the truth and changes the world and other characters instead of changing themselves |
| Ludic and narrative contracts | Hocking's pair: what the systems reward, and what the story asks the protagonist to value |
| Embedded and emergent narrative | Story placed in space for players to reconstruct, versus story arising from systems (Jenkins) |
| Front and grim portents | A tabletop method for an antagonist's offscreen plan: steps toward an impending doom that advance whether or not the players act |
| Pillar | A short, concrete statement of what the game is about, used to cut and keep content |
| Canon status | One tag per fact, such as settled, proposed, open or cut, so writers know what they may rely on |
| As-built story | The story the shipped or current build actually tells, reconstructed from data, as opposed to the story the design documents describe |
| Precondition and postcondition | The world state an action or beat requires, and the state it leaves (Doran & Parberry) |
| n+1 | Larian's rule that every plot-critical delivery has a fallback route for when the player has killed, skipped or broken the first one |
| Load-bearing and modular beats | Beats the climax depends on, versus colour that can be cut or reordered without breaking the causal chain |

## Cause, value turns and enacted theme are the best-sourced construction rules

Every popular structure model (three acts, Paul Gulino's eight sequences, John Truby's seven steps, Dan Harmon's story circle, Save the Cat, the Hero's Journey, kishōtenketsu) is a practitioner's description of patterns that recur in successful stories. None has been validated as a recipe, and no controlled study shows that following one improves a story. Their real value is diagnostic. Underneath the different names they test for the same functions: a status quo with a lack, a disruption, a goal and plan, escalating opposition, a crisis or lowest point, a climactic choice, and a changed equilibrium. The best-documented danger is using a model as a template. Peter Suderman found Blake Snyder's beats in order, and close to their page targets, in *Jack the Giant Slayer*, *Star Trek Into Darkness*, *Monsters University* and *The Avengers*, and argued that scenes written to hit a beat feel arbitrary and produce déjà vu. He conceded the beat sheet is useful as one tool among several ([Slate](https://www.slate.com/articles/arts/culturebox/2013/07/hollywood_and_blake_snyder_s_screenwriting_book_save_the_cat.html)). Folklorists have dismissed Campbell's monomyth as assembled from the stories that fit it ([Wikipedia: Hero's journey](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Monomyth)), and a DiGRA paper argues that most game-industry talk of "the hero's journey" really refers to Christopher Vogler's 1985 studio memo ([DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2972/2956/3015)). The popular claim that kishōtenketsu is "plot without conflict" overstates it. The defensible version is that it is not driven by one central confrontation, and its turn (*ten*) recontextualises rather than clashes ([Wikipedia: Kishōtenketsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish%C5%8Dtenketsu)). Ursula K. Le Guin's "carrier bag" essay offers a legitimate alternative shape for gathering-driven stories, a container of collected things and relations rather than a spear-shaped hero plot ([Le Guin](https://wiki.xxiivv.com/docs/carrier_bag.html)). For an exploration game the audit question then becomes what the gathering adds up to, not where the climactic fight is.

The rule with the deepest pedigree is causal linkage. Trey Parker and Matt Stone, as surprise guests in Ken Liotti's storytelling class at NYU in 2011, told students that if "and then" belongs between two beats the story is in trouble, and that the word between beats should be "therefore" or "but". The wording reaches us through press write-ups of the filmed class rather than a transcript ([Cheatsheet](https://www.cheatsheet.com/entertainment/south-park-trey-parker-and-matt-stone-1-rule.html/); [Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/the-book-of-mormon-writers-surprise-students-at-nyu-com-182431)). E. M. Forster made the same distinction between "the king died, and then the queen died" and adding "of grief", and Aristotle ranked episodic plots, where events merely follow one another, as the worst kind ([Wikipedia: Plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plot_(narrative))). Robert McKee supplies the unit test: a scene is an action through conflict that turns a value-charged condition in a character's life, and a scene whose value ends where it began is a non-event and a candidate for cutting ([McKee](https://mckeestory.com/do-your-scenes-turn/)). A constructed example shows the repair. The weak outline reads: Mira reaches the port, *and then* meets a smuggler, *and then* the city is attacked, *and then* she finds the relic. The repaired outline reads: Mira reaches the port, *but* the harbourmaster has sealed it, *therefore* she bargains with a smuggler, *but* his price is the relic map, *therefore* she steals it from the archive, *but* the theft trips the wards that let the raiders in. The attack is now the consequence of her choice instead of a coincidence, and every beat can be checked by asking whether any later beat would change if it were deleted.

Escalation and stakes have practitioner consensus behind them and one adjacent strand of evidence. On Writing Excuses, Mary Robinette Kowal describes try-fail cycles in which each solution creates the next problem, and Wesley Chu makes the case that a world-ending threat stays abstract until it is tied to a character, so the world ending means that one particular child never grows up. The same episode advises keeping the *same* stake while making failure worse, and sets homework that bans a loved one's death as the stakes source ("no fridging") ([Writing Excuses 12.41](https://writingexcuses.com/12-41-raising-the-stakes/)). Brandon Sanderson's 2025 lectures warn that a jump from nobody to saving the world within a few chapters makes stakes feel unearned ([Sanderson](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/brandon-sandersons-2025-overview-of-story-structure-lecture-3)). The evidence comes from charity research, not fiction. Small, Loewenstein and Slovic found that people give more to an identifiable victim than to statistical ones, that **adding statistics to an identifiable victim reduced giving**, and that teaching people about the bias cut giving to the identifiable victim without raising it for the statistical ones ([Stafforini abstract of *OBHDP* 102(2)](https://stafforini.com/works/small-2007-sympathy-callousness-impact/); [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/increase-charitable-donations-appeal-heart-not-head)). By analogy, a bible should anchor every large threat in one character the player has met and the concrete thing that character loses, and should not stack casualty counts on top of that face.

Setups and payoffs are where game stories most often break their own promises. Chekhov's letter of 1 November 1889 warns against putting a loaded rifle on stage if no one means to fire it, and the familiar pistol version comes from Ilia Gurliand's reminiscence, **published in 1904**, of an earlier conversation ([Wikipedia: Chekhov's gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chekhov%27s_gun)). Chekhov broke the rule himself, since *The Cherry Orchard* holds two firearms that never fire, so the working version is a ledger: every prominent setup is paid off, cut, or deliberately acknowledged. Sanderson frames the same discipline as promise, progress and payoff, including tonal promises made in the opening ([Sanderson](https://brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/brandon-sandersons-2025-guide-to-plot-lecture-2)). Emma Coats's 2011 tweet that coincidences getting characters *into* trouble are great while coincidences getting them *out* are cheating is the cleanest check on endings ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/the-22-rules-of-storytelling-according-to-pixar-5916970)). Coats was a Pixar story artist sharing lessons she had learned, and bloggers relabelled her tweets "Pixar's 22 rules" ([Prolost](https://prolost.com/blog/2013/12/4/pixars-22-rules-of-story-analyzed.html)), so the skill should credit her, not the studio. Aristotle's objection to the *deus ex machina* points the same way: the unravelling must arise from the plot ([Wikipedia: Deus ex machina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deus_ex_machina)). In a game the natural setups are the player's verbs and acquired abilities, so every exit from a major jam should use a capability, relationship or choice the player already has.

Theme belongs in action and outcome, not in anyone's mouth. Lajos Egri's premise is a qualified value in conflict leading to an outcome ("great love conquers even death"), McKee's controlling idea names the value that changes at the climax and the cause of that change, and Truby treats theme as a moral argument carried by the hero's choices, warning that a story becomes a sermon when the argument outweighs the plot ([Screenplayology](https://www.screenplayology.com/?p=22); [Shortform on McKee](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-a-controlling-idea/); [Truby notes](https://medium.com/@pirangy/balancing-moral-argument-with-plot-the-verdict-and-the-iliad-john-truby-the-anatomy-of-story-p-fa5426906a71)). Andrew Stanton's TED talk compresses the delivery rule into "give them two plus two", letting the audience assemble the meaning ([Singju Post transcript](https://singjupost.com/?p=2871)). Snyder's "Theme Stated" beat, an offhand line around page five spoken to the hero by someone else, is the documented exception ([StudioBinder](https://studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-save-the-cat-screenwriting)). A STORY.md can therefore record a theme *question* with two defensible answers, a controlling idea that the climax must enact and nobody states aloud, and at least one major character who embodies the opposing answer through what they do. If the game branches, each ending needs its own controlling idea, and each must answer the same question.

### The order of telling is a design layer of its own

The order in which events happen (fabula) and the order in which the audience learns them (syuzhet) are separate layers, and the second sets the emotion ([Wikipedia: Fabula and syuzhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabula_and_syuzhet)). Brewer and Lichtenstein's structural-affect experiments operationalise the choices. Postponing an outcome produces suspense, showing an outcome before its causes produces curiosity, and an unexpected event produces surprise ([AAAI-82 paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1982/AAAI82-063.pdf)). The curiosity component has since been questioned, on the grounds that the structural manipulation alone neither guarantees nor is required for curiosity ([Fry, JCLA 2025](https://jcla.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/JCLA-48.2_Summer-2025_Leslie-T.-Fry.pdf)). Loewenstein's information-gap model explains why an open question pulls a reader forward ([Golman & Loewenstein](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/Information%20Gaps--Golman%20and%20Loewenstein.pdf)). Hitchcock's bomb under the table is the classic argument for giving the audience the threat in advance, which turns a dull conversation into sustained suspense, with surprise reserved for cases where the reveal *is* the payoff ([Futility Closet](https://www.futilitycloset.com/2023/12/20/hitchcocks-bomb-theory/); [Bordwell](https://davidbordwell.net/blog/2013/11/29/hitchcock-lessing-and-the-bomb-under-the-table/trackback/)). A fair twist meets Aristotle's standard of being contrary to expectation yet caused by what came before. The spoiler research does not license ignoring twists. Leavitt and Christenfeld found spoiled stories enjoyed more, but Levine, Betzner and Autry, and Johnson and Rosenbaum, found spoilers *reduced* enjoyment ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/spoiler-alert-stories-are-not-spoiled-byspoilers.html); [OnFiction](https://www.onfiction.ca/2017/04/research-bulletin-spoiler-alert-not-all.html)). The safe rule is that a twist may never be a story's *only* source of engagement. A bible that keeps the fabula and the reveal order apart can also name the intended effect of every reordering, and can check each twist for at least two earlier beats that support it on a second pass.

### Arc shapes are planning vocabulary, not a quality test

Reagan and colleagues computed sentiment arcs for 1,327 Project Gutenberg books and found six recurring shapes: rags to riches, riches to rags, man in a hole, Icarus, Cinderella and Oedipus. The most common shapes were not the most downloaded. The three most successful were **Icarus, Oedipus and a double man-in-a-hole (fall, rise, fall, rise)**, not the simple man in a hole that is often quoted ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1606.07772)). The authors warn that downloads are only a rough proxy for success, that an emotional arc is not a plot, and that their method cannot see short emotional beats. Related sentiment-arc tools have documented smoothing artefacts ([Underwood](https://tedunderwood.com/2015/03/24/why-its-hard-for-syuzhet-to-be-right-or-wrong-yet/)). Toubia, Berger and Eliashberg modelled texts as paths through semantic space, and Berger reports that faster movement helped films and TV episodes while covering more ground hurt TV episodes; the direction of the third measure, circuitousness, is unverified ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-science-of-blockbusters-what-makes-a-good-story/)). The shapes are useful for naming an intended arc (a tragic Icarus for a companion, a falling Oedipus for a villain). The actionable audit stays at the level of McKee's per-unit value turn, which players feel and sentiment curves miss. The shapes matter for a second reason covered later: language models overwhelmingly produce one of them.

## Games are mostly Act 2, so story moves into verbs, spaces and loops

### Serial structure fits play better than three acts

Game writers inherited the three-act model and have argued against it from experience rather than data. Evan Skolnick told GDC 2007 that the typical game is almost entirely Act 2, with Acts 1 and 3 reduced to cutscene decoration. He mapped the Hero's Journey onto games but skipped the "refusal of the call", because a hero who refuses the call is a player asked whether to keep playing, and he stressed that such structures describe rather than prescribe ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-evan-skolnick-asks-game-writers-to-make-it-snappier-)). Tom Abernathy and Richard Rouse III's "Death to the Three Act Structure!" (GDC 2014) proposed serialised television as the better model: a season arc carried by relationships, plus episodes that stand alone, as in Telltale's *The Walking Dead* and the seasonal sections of *The Last of Us* ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020050/Death-to-the-Three-Act); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-plot-is-overrated-game-narrative-is-all-about-your-characters)). Their emphasis on characters over plot, and David Kuelz's rebuttal, are covered in the dialogue report. Gulino's sequences, each a mini-story with its own question and partial resolution ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/8-sequence-structure/)), map naturally onto chapters, regions or run tiers. Henry Jenkins's "accordion" structure, in which plot points stay fixed and the middles expand, says the same thing in spatial terms ([MIT text](https://web.mit.edu/~21fms/People/henry3/games&narrative.html)). Taken together, the sources point to a bible whose macro-structure is a short list of fixed, load-bearing turns, with chapter-sized stretches between them that each carry their own question, escalation and payoff.

### The verbs carry the theme or contradict it

Clint Hocking's 2007 critique of BioShock remains the canonical case of mechanics and story making opposite promises. The ludic contract said "seek power and you will progress", rewarding the player for harvesting Little Sisters, while the narrative contract said "help Atlas and you will progress" and gave no way to refuse him. When the mind-control reveal arrived, Hocking wrote that "the game openly mocks us", and he nearly quit in protest ([Hocking](https://clicknothing.com/2007/10/07/ludonarrative-d/)). The documented successes run the other way: the theme was found in the verbs, often because the mechanics came first. Lucas Pope called Papers, Please a design-first project, built bottom-up from what an inspector faces, in which each new mechanic needs a story reason (a body scanner makes no sense until there has been a terrorist attack or smuggling), and in which adding a single encounter or rule would disrupt the schedule of the rest of the game ([Reason](https://reason.com/2013/09/26/papers-please-politics-in-games-and-the/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)). Toby Fox said Undertale's mercy system grew out of enjoying talking to monsters in Shin Megami Tensei and from a wish to question what games take for granted ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-undertale-i-makes-you-think-hard-before-killing-monsters)). Outer Wilds removed collectibles and upgrades so that knowledge is the only progression and curiosity the only reason to explore ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)), and Hades turned dying, the genre's core frustration, into the story's engine ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)). Journey shows that a mechanic can tell the wrong story. In a game about companionship, early players could grab shared flight cloth and shove each other around, so the team removed collision and made nearby players recharge each other. The fix was to the systems, not to the writing ([Adventure Gamers](https://adventuregamers.com/article/designing_journey?cp=2)). Dissonance can be the subject, as Walt Williams argued for Spec Ops: The Line at GDC 2013, but only when the story acknowledges it ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1017980/We-Are-Not-Heroes-Contextualizing)). Three checks follow. The bible states both contracts in one sentence each and resolves any opposition or names the beat that confronts it. Each major theme maps to at least one verb, rule or world-state response, not just to cutscenes. And for each theme the bible lists any cheap affordance that lets the player act against it.

### Each delivery mode makes its own demand on the bible

Jenkins's four modes of spatial narrative give the vocabulary: *evoked* (a space calls up a story players already know), *enacted* (players perform events), *embedded* (story is scattered for players to reconstruct, the world as a memory palace) and *emergent* (story arises from systems) ([MIT text](https://web.mit.edu/~21fms/People/henry3/games&narrative.html)). Jenkins cites an existing "law of three", from soap-opera practice: any essential plot point should be conveyed in at least three ways, because players will miss clues. Drawing on Kevin Lynch, he also warns that a landscape in which every rock tells a story leaves players no room to make their own. Environmental storytelling has two canonical practitioner sources. Don Carson's 2000 article ported theme-park practice to games: set a governing story the whole team follows, never break the world's rules, and answer "Where am I?" within about 15 seconds ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/environmental-storytelling-creating-immersive-3d-worlds-using-lessons-learned-from-the-theme-park-industry)). Harvey Smith and Matthias Worch's "What Happened Here?" (GDC 2010) asks for a readable chain of events that echoes the world's premise, and names as a pitfall any staged scene implying actions the player could never perform ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1012647); [slides](https://slidetodoc.com/level-design-workshop-what-happened-here-environmental-storytelling/)). Fragmented lore has a documented risk. A long-time FromSoftware translator suggested that much Souls lore probably exists only in Miyazaki's imagination rather than in a written canon ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/action-rpg/long-time-dark-souls-and-elden-ring-dev-thinks-miyazaki-stores-the-entire-lore-in-his-head)), and Team Cherry found that secrets they expected to take weeks to uncover were found within an hour, with dataminers exposing everything ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-makers-of-hollow-knight-are-fine-with-players-missi-1828721254)). Knowledge-gated design adds its own lesson. Outer Wilds' early opening crashed an object into the village, and it fell flat because players did not yet know the village; the shipped opening has players collect launch codes first ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)). Run-based story needs a fiction for death and repetition. Hades checks run state (for example, meeting a character while Zagreus is below a health threshold), serves one of several written events with designer weights, and fixes the sequencing of only a couple of moments ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)). Emily Short adds that a "loop and grow" structure delivers repeated encounters and growth more reliably than branching, and that a loop which degrades with each pass suits tragedy ([Short thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1388979576248967169.html)). Each mode therefore asks the bible for something specific: embedded lore needs the complete hidden truth plus a ledger of which fragment reveals what and where; environmental scenes need a per-vignette chain of events limited to outcomes the game's systems could produce; knowledge gates need a dependency graph with more than one route to each gate; and run-based stories need eligibility rules, per-visit caps and an ending gated on narrative state rather than on a single win. Because players and dataminers will read everything, optional lore must never contradict canon.

### The protagonist type is a structural decision

Whether the player character speaks is a story-structure decision, not a voice decision. Nintendo tried giving Link lines in Echoes of Wisdom, found that it "felt really wrong", and built a setting in which he cannot speak ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/link-was-going-to-speak-in-zelda-echoes-of-wisdom-but-nintendo-thought-it-didnt-feel-right)). Marc Laidlaw said Gordon Freeman's internal story can only be told through the way other characters treat him, which is why Alyx works: she gives the player back emotion ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/laidlaw-i-half-life-2-i-s-alyx-works-because-she-gives-you-back-emotion-)). Defined protagonists allow authored arcs but open a gap between what the character wants and what the player wants. Neil Druckmann acknowledged that The Last of Us's fixed ending "rubbed some people the wrong way" and credited the opening for aligning the player with Joel first ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/the-last-of-us-creators-compare-their-blockbuster-games-beginning-and-ending/2)). Amnesia has no developer defence on record. Critics consistently describe it as an exposition crutch the player sees through, since the player is not amnesiac ([GameBanshee](https://www.gamebanshee.com/df4q)). Player-created heroes leave a hole at the centre unless an origin fills it. Divinity: Original Sin 2's custom characters lacked their own story, and Baldur's Gate 3's Dark Urge answered with a customisable origin that has a past to uncover ([Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/news/baldurs-gate-3-will-fix-one-of-divinity-2s-biggest-flaws-3985098); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/baldurs-gate-3-dark-urge)). The corresponding checks: a silent hero has no beat that requires them to voice a feeling; a defined hero has, per act, a stated want and player objective and a note on whether they agree; a player-made hero's spine does not depend on backstory unless an origin supplies one; and a per-beat ledger of what the protagonist knows against what the player knows replaces amnesia with a newcomer protagonist or a knowledgeable companion.

### Every signalled choice is a promise the ending has to keep

The documented story-level failures are less about the number of branches than about promises left unpaid. Mass Effect 3's ending was seen as not honouring three games of choices. BioWare's free Extended Cut of June 2012 added cinematics and epilogues showing how choices played out, and BioWare said it "does not fundamentally change the endings", though it did add a fourth option, refusing the Catalyst ([BioWare blog](https://blog.bioware.com/2012/06/22/mass-effect-3-extended-cut-2/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Effect_3_ending_controversy)). Hades II shows the second kind of broken promise. Its original ending gave the climactic compassionate choice to Zagreus rather than to the protagonist, Melinoë. Supergiant changed this in **Patch 1, about a month after the 1.0 release of 25 September 2025**, so that Melinoë makes the call, and Kasavin said the team decided "really quickly" ([OpenCritic/TheGamer](https://opencritic.com/news/22553/hades-2s-latest-update-has-changed-the-true-ending-that-nobody-liked); [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/hades-2-supergiant-narrative-interview-ending-changes/)). Kentucky Route Zero shows that a game can avoid the problem by making no consequence promise at all, offering expressive choices that shape character rather than outcome ([Radiator](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2016/12/on-legacy-systems-and-kentucky-route.html)). Storylets and inkle's "defensive logic" for the open-world Sorcery! games keep a story coherent whatever order the player takes ([Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/); [Xsolla summary of Ingold](https://accelerator.xsolla.com/blog/narrative-sorcery-coherent-storytelling-in-an-open-world)). Telltale's unpaid "will remember that" is in the dialogue report. Two checks follow from these cases. A promise ledger lists every choice the game signals as consequential, in UI, origin choice or marketing, with at least one visible payoff each, and the endings reflect the choices the game made biggest. And the climax is performed or chosen by the player character, unless the bible justifies withholding it as the point and has aligned the player with the protagonist first.

## Most players never see the ending, so pacing is a design problem

### Completion data puts the meaning before the midpoint

Trophy and achievement data, which measures reaching an ending rather than understanding a story, consistently shows that most players stop early. MIDiA found that Assassin's Creed Valhalla's roughly 61-hour campaign was finished by **15–17%** of players, against about **50%** for shorter games such as Spider-Man and The Last of Us ([MIDiA](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/gamers-arent-finishing-games-and-thats-a-problem)). A 2008 Microsoft panel of about 14,000 Xbox Live players found that about **30% did not finish even the 13 most popular games** of the year, in a sample the author believed skewed hardcore ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/xbox-live-gamerscore-completion-stats-show-major-trends)). An academic study of 412 Steam games with a main-path completion achievement found completion between 0% and 56%, with a **mean of 14% and a median of 10%**; the authors call it preliminary ([Bailey & Miyata, ICEC 2017](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-66715-7_65)). A columnist's dataset of 100 top-rated 2023–24 games found only a weak negative correlation between length and completion (r = −0.24), so shortening a game does not by itself fix the problem ([Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.de/en/page/hardly-anyone-finishes-games-so-why-are-they-so-long-37002)). The implication for a bible is that the central question, the main relationship and the theme must be legible by the midpoint of the beat map, and at least one emotional payoff must land in the first session. The ending is for the minority who arrive, not the only place the meaning lives. A story whose every payoff sits in the last act is also the most exposed to schedule cuts, as the diagnosis section shows.

### Story works as a reward, in many short segments

Practitioners agree that story should arrive while play happens or as a reward after it, not as a toll before it. Valve's Erik Wolpaw dislikes games stopping so a writer can tell a story and asks to be told "as the game happens" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-austin-i-half-life-i-i-portal-i-scribes-talk-writing-process)). Sony's writing guide reports that F.I.S.T.: Forged in Shadow Torch split its plot into about **50 segments across roughly 20 hours**, after the producer watched players put the controller down "to eat chips" when given too much story at once ([Sony Interactive](https://sonyinteractive.com/en/writing-for-video-games-part-1/)). That is the only concrete segmenting figure in the research, and no published standard exists for cutscene length, story minutes per hour or beat spacing. The documented planning tool is the beat chart, which Scott Rogers's *Level Up!* borrowed from film to spot gaps and clumping ([Packt](https://subscription.packtpub.com/book/game-development/9781118877166/22/ch22lvl1sec81/youve-got-the-beat)). Nick Filatov's version has one row per location, with columns for description, new mechanic, story events, player progress, time to complete, content (characters, items, threats) and audio, and it is scanned for overload, gaps and repeated combinations ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/beat-chart-game-designer-s-best-friend)). Emotional peaks need playtesting rather than script reading. After about two years of Journey's development a playtester said the game should end after the mountain; Jenova Chen concluded the final high did not rise far enough above the preceding low, made the mountain harder and opened the summit to free play, and after an extra year 3 of 25 testers had tears in their eyes at the end ([Adventure Gamers](https://adventuregamers.com/article/designing_journey?cp=2)). Accessibility guidance adds a floor. Xbox Accessibility Guideline 109 asks for objectives reviewable at any time, a log of completed objectives, a way to revisit the story (cutscene replay or a written story-so-far) and descriptive saves, citing Dragon Quest XI S's start-up recap and Tell Me Why's cutscene repository ([XAG 109](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/109)). A beat map built from these sources gives every row a story beat, a mechanic or progression change and a reason in the fiction linking them, flags rows that stack a major tutorial on a major exposition beat, places non-interactive beats after accomplishments or over low-demand traversal, and marks the intended emotional low and high so that no second-act peak outshines the finale.

### Channel choice trades control against attention

Every delivery channel trades authorial control against the player's attention, and practitioners keep anything critical off optional channels. Outer Wilds' Kelsey Beachum put it as a ranking: found text should be interesting, but not as interesting as being sucked into a cyclone ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/explaining-the-value-of-show-don-t-tell-storytelling-in-i-outer-wilds-i-)). God of War (2018) presented the whole game as one apparently unbroken shot against heavy internal resistance, and Cory Barlog said 40% of the team came round only after playing the finished game ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/barlog-i-god-of-war-i-s-single-shot-camera-trick-was-a-tough-sell-for-devs)). The table below is a synthesis from the sources in this section, not a published standard.

| Channel | Best for | Avoid for |
|---|---|---|
| Cutscene with an authored camera | Irreversible turns, deaths, reveals that need framing | Exposition the player could learn by playing; anything before first control |
| Continuous in-engine scene | Relationships, walk-and-talk, tension | Long information dumps |
| Environment and props | Backstory, world history, "what happened here" | Facts the player must know to proceed, unless repeated elsewhere |
| Item, codex and found text | Optional depth and mystery for the curious | Core plot and the protagonist's main motivation |
| Narration and radio | Commentary that reacts to play | Moments that need visual drama |
| Systems (world state, music, who is present) | Consequences of player conduct, theme | Precise information |
| Recaps and summaries | Returning players and accessibility | Replacing delivery in play |

### Narrative that joins late inherits a fixed story space

Rhianna Pratchett told DICE 2016 that writers are often hired after the game is designed, as "narrative paramedics" asked to wrap a story around it ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/rhianna-pratchett-stop-bringing-in-writers-too-late-during-development/)). Even where story is central, built things come first. Half-Life 2's opening train ride came from a programmer's train tool ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-austin-i-half-life-i-i-portal-i-scribes-talk-writing-process)), and one former Naughty Dog designer recalls Uncharted 3 starting from about a dozen planned set pieces that the story then had to absorb ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/the-success-of-uncharted-2s-set-pieces-had-a-negative-impact-on-uncharted-3-designer-says/)). Amy Hennig's counter-rule is that writers must know where act breaks, obstacles, reversals and set pieces fall ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/amy-hennig-discusses-new-star-wars-game-it-sounds-/1100-6441881/)). The best-documented late-change failure is Destiny, covered in the diagnosis section. Writers and narrative designers divide the work differently by studio. Molly Maloney and Eric Stirpe put it as the writer owning the characters and the narrative designer owning the player's experience of the story ([Wikipedia: Narrative designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrative_designer)). For the skill, two consequences follow. Beats should be tied to *functions* and explicit dependencies ("beat 7 requires the player to know X from beat 3"), not only to locations, so that missions can be reordered without breaking causality. And an audit should begin as a late-joining narrative designer does, by inventorying the available story space (cutscene slots, voice budget, environments, channels) before proposing anything.

## Characters are wants under pressure, and worlds are rules that cost something

### A character core is one want, one weakness, one belief and one pressured choice

The character frameworks agree on a small core, though several are known here only through reader notes rather than the books. Truby's seven steps begin with *weakness and need*, then *desire*, and separate a psychological weakness that hurts only the hero from a moral one that hurts others, with one primary desire line per protagonist ([Medium summary](https://medium.com/@pirangy/the-seven-key-steps-of-story-structure-john-truby-the-anatomy-of-story-d04a9c60031f)). K. M. Weiland's chain runs from a wound to a Lie the character believes, with the Want as the conscious goal and the Need as the truth that disproves the Lie, often reached only by giving up the Want ([Weiland](https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/character-arcs-3/)). Egri's three dimensions (physiology, sociology, psychology) are notes the writer must know but should surface through behaviour ([Goodreads summary](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/6058330-story-and-the-dimensions-of-character?tab=author)), and McKee separates surface characterisation from true character, which shows only in choices made under pressure ([Writers Store excerpt](https://writersstore.com/blogs/news/structure-and-character-excerpted-with-permission-from-the-book-story-part-one)). The best-documented risk is the trauma backstory used as a universal explanation. Parul Sehgal's 2021 essay argues that trauma has become the all-purpose motive in contemporary fiction and reduces character to symptom, and her critics counter that the label gets attached to fiction with nothing at its centre, so the target is trauma used as explanation, not trauma as subject ([The Millions](https://themillions.com/2022/01/an-examination-of-the-trauma-plot.html)). The working test is deletion: if removing the backstory leaves present behaviour unexplained, it is structural; if it only explains a trait the player already sees, it is decoration. Specific wounds beat generic ones. Greg Kasavin built Hades' Demeter from one myth, a mother who lost this particular daughter, and that single fact shaped her bitterness and the angle of her cold powers ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-added-new-goddess-demeter-to-i-hades-i-pantheon)). Studios test characters off the page: Riot wrote internal short stories to find Kindred, and Barlog wrote a hunting-trip story so the God of War team could feel the father and son together (both in the dialogue report). No study measures what makes a game character memorable, so the sourced proxies are the ones a bible can record: a specific want visible in play, a weakness typed as psychological or moral, a Lie or Doubt matching the arc, an optional and specific past cause, the feeling the player should have, and one on-screen choice under pressure.

### Games put the arc on the cast, not the hero

Weiland's taxonomy distinguishes a positive arc (Lie to Truth), a flat arc (the hero holds the Truth and changes others, under a Doubt that never becomes insuperable) and three negative arcs: disillusionment, fall and corruption ([Weiland](https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/learn-5-types-of-character-arc-at-a-glance-the-3-negative-arcs-part-2-of-2/); [Weiland on flat arcs](https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/doubt-key-flat-character-arcs/)). Her flat-arc examples, such as Captain America across his films and Thor in his second film, are returning heroes, and she notes that a flat main arc can carry a change arc in a subplot. Flat arcs suit games whenever the protagonist is silent, customisable, a returning series hero or a repeating roguelite runner. The change then moves to companions who each hold a version of the Lie, to a faction, or to the antagonist, and the bible should name who holds the Lie and where each is converted, broken or lost. Where the protagonist does have a change arc, at least one player choice should be able to push it, or it is a cutscene arc the player watches happen to someone else. Insomniac's Spider-Man gives a worked corruption arc. Otto Octavius begins as Peter's mentor and father figure and turns around the second-act break, and an analysis of the game argues the played relationship is what gives the turn its weight, so any planned betrayal needs positive played beats first ([Barelli, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/insomniac-s-spider-man-building-a-powerful-antagonist)).

### An ensemble is a web of different answers to one question

Truby treats a cast as a web defined by function, archetype, theme and opposition, with roles such as the fake-ally opponent and the fake-opponent ally ([Goodreads reader notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/213909983-the-anatomy-of-story/82155820-a-j-mcmahon?page=7)). His "four-corner opposition", in secondary summaries, sets a hero against a main opponent and at least two secondary opponents, all in conflict with one another and made as different as possible, a society in miniature ([Turning to Story](https://turningtostory.substack.com/p/four-corner-opposition)). Game studios add practical checks. In a GDC 2023 roundtable Mike Laidlaw described dividing Dragon Age companions among writers and building each to react to the game's central conflict with a different opinion, so that no companion stood in for a single philosophy ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/mike-laidlaw-explains-how-the-dragon-age-writers-room-tackled-companions); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mike-laidlaw-on-why-you-can-t-romance-varric-in-dragon-age)). Kasavin said archetypal personalities let players tell a large cast apart at once, and that a new character has to serve the story and fit the existing cast ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-added-new-goddess-demeter-to-i-hades-i-pantheon)); Hades' cast shares one frame, a big dysfunctional family, with each member taking a different stance within it ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/black-athena-how-hades-gets-its-gods-right-1845146132)). Persona's Confidants tie befriending supporting characters to perks in other systems, from fusion bonuses to shop discounts and battle skills ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/s-links-to-confidants-an-evolution-of-character-interaction-)), and Mass Effect 2's loyalty missions resolve a squadmate's personal conflict and decide whether they survive the finale ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/mass-effect-andromeda-will-help-you-get-to-know-your-crew-with-the-return-of-loyalty-missions)). Josh Sawyer credits New Vegas's factions with feeling real because their members disagree and challenge each other ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/fallout/fallout-new-vegas-director-josh-sawyer-says-the-ncr-and-caesars-legion-feel-so-real-because-their-members-disagree-and-challenge-each-other-you-have-to-wrestle-with-the-fact-that-it-contains-multitudes/)). From these a bible can run a stance grid: state the theme as a question, give each core character a one-line answer and a method, and merge or change any two who answer the same way by the same method. Each companion also needs a want independent of the main quest, at least one likely player choice they will oppose, an opinion of at least one other companion (a web rather than spokes to the player), an arc beat the player can affect, and a mechanical hook. No source gives a recommended cast size, so the bible should tier every named character (core, recurring, functional, ambient) and scale the core cast to the content budget per character.

### Antagonists share the hero's goal and attack the hero's weakness

The best-sourced antagonist rule is Truby's: the strongest opponent wants the same goal as the hero and is exceptionally good at attacking the hero's greatest weakness ([Barnett's notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/213909983-the-anatomy-of-story/1477637-bill-barnett/73499de1-9340-4a09-b224-5f643e742f4d)). Barelli's analysis of Spider-Man applies it. Otto and Peter both want justice for New York and Norman Osborn held to account, but Otto will sacrifice innocents and Peter will not, so Otto's plan turns Peter's morality into a liability: Peter keeps saving strangers instead of chasing the antidote. The same game gives an impersonal threat, the Devil's Breath plague, a face by making it Otto's weapon ([Barelli](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/insomniac-s-spider-man-building-a-powerful-antagonist)). Tabletop game-masters supply the clearest method for a villain who acts offscreen. A Dungeon World front pairs each danger with an impending doom and a sequence of grim portents that advance whether or not the players act: one to three portents for an adventure front, three to five for a campaign front, and at most three dangers per front ([Roll20](https://roll20.net/compendium/dw/Fronts)). Sly Flourish advises keeping the doom sinister but short of world-ending so players don't feel they have no choice ([Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/fronts_in_dnd.html)). Rivals show how audience taste shifts. Junichi Masuda gave three reasons Pokémon rivals became friendlier: pixel graphics had pushed personality into harsher dialogue, HD would make a jerk rival feel far more intense, and, in his own view, players accept such personalities less than before ([My Nintendo News](https://mynintendonews.com/2018/10/22/junichi-masuda-explains-why-your-rivals-in-the-pokemon-franchise-arent-jerks-anymore/)). Critics answered that friendly, easily beaten rivals lost their tension ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2018/07/game-freak-how-many-more-friendly-pokeman-rivals-suffer-through/)). Constant presence wears a villain out too. Anthony Burch's first draft of Borderlands 2's Handsome Jack joked even through personal tragedy and grew tiresome, so later passes gave him a breaking point ([GameBanshee](https://www.gamebanshee.com/uakq)). The antagonist sheet that follows from these sources names the shared goal and the opposing method, the hero weakness exploited, a one-line counter-argument on the theme that a reasonable player could find tempting, three to five portents with what happens at each if the player does nothing, and, for an abstract threat, the person or faction who wields, worships or profits from it. As the model-failure section shows, a language model left to itself will soften every one of these fields.

### World rules earn their place by costing someone something

Brandon Sanderson's laws treat world rules as tools whose limits create plot. His First Law, from a 2007 essay, holds that an author's ability to resolve conflict with magic is directly proportional to how well the reader understands it, and the same essay places soft and hard magic at opposite ends of a continuum ([Sanderson](https://brandonsanderson.com/sandersons-first-law/)). His Second Law, "Limitations > Powers", separates limitations (what the magic cannot do), weaknesses (what enemies exploit) and costs (what it takes from the user), and his Third Law says to expand what exists before adding something new ([Sanderson FAQ](https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/what-are-sandersons-laws-of-magic/); [Second Law](https://brandonsanderson.com/sandersons-second-law/)). M. John Harrison's 2007 attack on worldbuilding as "the great clomping foot of nerdism" argues that it literalises invention and does the reader's imaginative work for them ([Gwern archive](https://gwern.net/doc/fiction/fantasy/2007-01-27-mjohnharrison-veryafraid-criticismofsffworldbuildingandtolkiensubcreationtheory.html); [3 Quarks Daily](https://3quarksdaily.com/?p=139062)). The synthesis that survives both is Hemingway's iceberg: know far more than you show, and put on screen only the rules that create a decision or a conflict. Every rule in a bible can therefore name the limitation or cost that creates a decision for some character, and say whether the player must understand it to solve something; if so, it must be taught before it resolves anything, or the climax becomes what commentators call the "eagle solution". Factions need goals that are incompatible with at least one other faction's, a contested resource, internal dissent and a stance on the theme, the New Vegas test above. History earns space by naming the present pressure it creates (a grudge, a debt, a ruin, a taboo, a contested claim), and events that create none are tagged as optional deep lore. World of Warcraft's historians show why a canon check matters even for a big studio: their research sometimes forced planned stories to change, as when writers planned a storyline for a character who had already died ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/2018/08/14/a-look-inside-how-blizzard-maintains-world-of-warcrafts-lore)).

### Real cultures need named sources and a recorded consultation level

The documented good practice for real cultures, folklore and history is early, continuous and empowered involvement from the people depicted. Never Alone began with the Cook Inlet Tribal Council. About 40 Alaska Native elders, storytellers and community members contributed, the narration is entirely in Iñupiaq, and storyteller Ishmael Hope helped steer the developers toward authentic stories, though the Denver Art Museum says the development team chose the central Kunuuksaayuka story ([NPR Code Switch](https://text.npr.org/365791351); [Denver Art Museum](https://denverartmuseum.org/article/story-how-alaska-native-story-led-video-game)). When Rivers Were Trails is described as a "sovereign" game, directed by Indigenous creatives who kept the final decisions ([ICT News](https://ictnews.org/lifestyle/real-native-history-in-a-video-game-an-indigenous-take-on-the-oregon-trail/)). Sony's Japan team reviewed Ghost of Tsushima from the pitch onward and confirmed that every kanji shown existed in the 13th century, yet critics still found stereotype in places ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/sucker-punch-discusses-ghost-of-tsushimas-approach-to-cultural-authenticity)), and Ubisoft's statement that Assassin's Creed Shadows was never meant as factual history did not defuse its controversy ([TechXplore/AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2024-07-assassin-creed-makers-defend-creative.html)). Consultation lowers risk but certifies nothing. An agent cannot consult anyone, so what it can do is record, per borrowed culture, the specific source tradition (region, period, text or version), a consultation level on a ladder from desk research through insider review to insiders writing and community final say, the documented scholarly disagreements and deliberate departures, and the specific questions a consultant should answer. It should flag any culture whose consultation level is low for how central it is, and it should never mark anything "authentic". Mixing several real cultures into one fantasy faction without saying which element came from where is the pastiche pattern to flag.

### Names need a per-culture sound spec and a collision check

Two documented naming methods sit at opposite ends. Tolkien built languages first and invented stories to give them a world ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_constructed_by_Tolkien)). A. K. Larkwood's lighter method picks a consonant inventory, a syllable shape, cluster rules and one spelling per sound for each culture, so that, for example, the same sound is spelled c, k or q depending on who is speaking and readers feel the cultures apart ([Larkwood](https://aklarkwood.com/naming-language)). Editors' distinctiveness rules (no shared initials in the core cast, no rhymes or lookalikes, no first name echoing another character's surname) are heuristics that even some writers dispute, but they are cheap to check ([BookEnds](https://bookendsliterary.com/?p=1300)). Pokémon shows the localisation side. Tsunekazu Ishihara said each name was rebuilt one by one with no fixed rule to mean something in English (Bulbasaur from bulb and -saurus, Ekans as "snake" reversed), while Pikachu kept its name because the trademark was secured worldwide ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2016/03/tsunekazu_ishihara_on_the_difficulty_of_naming_pokemon_for_different_regions)). A bible can therefore hold a naming spec per culture that differs from every other culture on at least two dimensions, a meaning gloss for each invented creature or place name so localisers can rebuild it, and a collision check against the cast, famous IP, trademarks, offensive words in target languages and UI length limits. A failed naming rule is a stronger signal of genericness than a hit on any ban list, because model-default names (covered later) are exactly the ones that obey no setting's phonology.

## Tone is a contract with the core loop, and particulars come from the owner

### A tonal contract names a register, the loop it matches and the moments it protects

The games praised for mixing tones pick one dominant register, tie it to how the game plays, and protect certain moments from jokes. Kasavin told GameDaily that roguelikes have "an inherently slapstick, comedic" quality and that Hades' cast ranges from serious to silly, so the story's tone was meant to match the loop ([GameDaily](https://www.gamedaily.biz/?p=588)); Early Access feedback then confirmed the humour the team had been unsure of ([GeekDad](https://geekdad.com/2019/10/narrative-and-early-access-supergiants-greg-kasavin-discusses-hades-development/), via search-indexed text). Toby Fox says he checks that a serious scene isn't ruined by a joke, while admitting he may keep a really funny one on purpose, which makes protected moments a strong default rather than an absolute ban ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/deltarune-chapter-2-interview-toby-fox-development-characters)). Darkest Dungeon shows the dark version. Chris Bourassa described tuning crit chances, art, lighting and narration to one message, that the player is underpowered and vulnerable, with successes "commented on bemusingly" and the narrator saving his excitement for big hits. Tyler Sigman added that it is "not a game for everyone" and that committing to the tone strangely widened its appeal, a belief the team held before full release ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-darkest-dungeon-i-designing-for-despair-and-kicking-you-when-you-re-down)). Spiritfarer shows a light game carrying weight: the team's own memories of people who had died became the spirits' core identities, and the team met patients and carers in end-of-life care ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-)). Psychonauts 2 kept the series' wackiness while psychologist Raffael Boccamazzo reviewed its mental-health content ([Double Fine](https://www.doublefine.com/news/chatting-with-the-doctor)). The documented failures are mismatches. Critics of the Marvel quip argue that reflexive jokes tell the audience the characters don't care ([Escapist](https://escapistmagazine.com/black-widow-should-take-itself-more-seriously-humor-comedy-mcu)), an opinion rather than evidence. Forspoken's banter was drawn from "a handful of unique conversations" and repeated until a setting was added to reduce it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/23/forspoken-review-square-enixs-risky-new-ip-arrives-half-baked)), though its reception is contested and its register problem is covered in the dialogue report. Uncharted 4's hidden "Ludonarrative Dissonance" trophy for 1,000 kills names the gap between a charming hero and his body count ([GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/uncharted4/trophy-list/zb10505)). Television has a documented procedure for agreeing tone before production. In the WGA East guide, the writer leads a tone meeting, highlights the emotional and tonal moments in each scene and walks the director through them, and the guide warns that meetings over 90 minutes drag ([WGA East](https://www.wgaeast.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/02/THE-IDIOTS-GUIDE-TO-COVERING-SET.pdf)). A bible's tone section therefore needs a dominant register, the gameplay feel it matches, a list of protected moments, the story's explicit attitude to the main loop, a scene-by-scene tonal tag for major beats, and, for light games, at least one relationship-level loss treated with care, or for dark games, rationed triumphs, reversals and gallows wit.

### Acclaimed particulars came from lives, procedures and research

In every well-documented case, the specific material came first and the genre was fitted around it. Celeste's portrayal of anxiety came from Maddy Thorson's own, with details arriving as Thorson understood that anxiety better, and Kotaku reports no mental-health professionals were consulted ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692)). Firewatch is set in Wyoming's Shoshone National Forest in 1989, the year after the 1988 fires, and Sean Vanaman, who grew up in Cody, said living there inspired it ([Wyoming Public Media](https://www.wyomingpublicmedia.org/open-spaces/2014-08-08/new-video-game-will-be-set-in-wyoming-wilderness)). Night in the Woods' Possum Springs drew visually on western Pennsylvania towns such as Altoona, Johnstown and Lewistown, where Scott Benson moved at 18 ([Pittsburgh City Paper](https://www.pghcitypaper.com/news/video-game-night-in-the-woods-tells-a-mysterious-tale-of-western-pennsylvania-1981248), via search-indexed text). Papers, Please began with Lucas Pope, an American in Japan, watching what immigration inspectors did with paperwork ([Reason](https://reason.com/2013/09/26/papers-please-politics-in-games-and-the/)). Research and consultants are the third source. Pentiment's historical advisers included Edmund Kern and Winston Black ([Lawrence University](https://www.lawrence.edu/?p=13919)), and Ninja Theory approached Cambridge psychiatrist Paul Fletcher in 2013 with Wellcome funding and consulted people with lived experience through Recovery College East ([University of Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/hellblade)). Morteshka chose Slavic folklore over generic fantasy with elves and goblins and drew One-Eyed Likho from Afanasyev's 19th-century collection ([Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-one-eyed-likho-brings-slavic-folk-horror-to-life), via search-indexed text). On Writing Excuses, Kowal described building *Shades of Milk and Honey* to avoid the evil overlords her fantasy instincts suggested ([Writing Excuses 12.41](https://writingexcuses.com/12-41-raising-the-stakes/)). Chekhov's actual advice on concrete detail was a specific image, the neck of a broken bottle glittering on a mill dam, which later compressions turned into the misattributed "glint of light on broken glass" ([Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2013/07/30/moon-glint/)). The originality evidence is thin and mixed: one study of Billboard songs found moderate distinctiveness paid off, while a later, larger study found every kind of novelty measured associated with lower popularity ([Columbia](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/what-makes-popular-culture-popular-product-features-and-optimal-differentiation); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12943v1)). The aim is therefore *particularity* (specific, motivated, causally load-bearing detail) rather than novelty for its own sake. Because an agent has no lived experience, its job is to draw particulars out of the owner or point to research and consultants, and to mark any unsourced "specific" detail it invents as a placeholder until someone verifies it.

### Building on an inspiration means keeping the feeling and changing the expression

US copyright protects expression, not ideas. The Copyright Office says copyright "does not protect facts, ideas, systems, or methods of operation" but may protect how they are expressed ([USCO FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html)), 17 U.S.C. §102(b) excludes any idea, procedure, system, method of operation or concept ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102)), and Circular 33 adds that names, titles and short phrases are not copyrightable, though trademark law may protect them ([Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). Mechanics carry a different risk. Nintendo and The Pokémon Company sued Pocketpair in the Tokyo District Court on 18 September 2024 for patent infringement, not copyright ([Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/corporate/release/en/2024/240919.html)); Pocketpair disputes the patents and changed features in later updates, and as of 20 November 2026 no judgment or settlement has been reported ([Pocketpair](https://www.pocketpair.jp/en/news/regarding-the-lawsuit-changes-to-palworld-and-the-future/); [Games Fray](https://gamesfray.com/category/games-intellectual-property-rights/nintendo-v-pocketpair-palworld/)). Separately, Palworld's creature designs drew public accusations of tracing, a reputational cost whatever the legal outcome ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/palworld-pokemon-plagiarism-accusations-pile-up-as-ceo-responds)). The documented creative moves keep the feeling or feature and change the surface. Eric Barone wanted to recapture the special feeling of Harvest Moon, felt the series had got worse over time and saw nothing like it on PC ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-concernedape-s-i-stardew-valley-i-)). Cassette Beasts' Jay Baylis said Bytten was "not making a game meant to be a tribute or derivative" and believed theirs was the only game in the genre with fusion ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/features/cassette-beasts-dev-on-doing-what-pokemon-doesnt-in-a-zelda-inspired-overworld)). Team Cherry's William Pellen named Zelda II, Faxanadu, Super Metroid and Castlevania as influences, and for Silksong the team chased those games' sense of mystery rather than their look ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/when-we-made-hollow-knight/); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action/silksong-devs-say-its-very-difficult-to-preserve-mystery-these-days-and-zelda-2-era-inspirations-pushed-them-toward-a-world-you-can-explore-divorced-from-the-internet)). Yacht Club documented the NES rules Shovel Knight deliberately broke, from sprite flicker to colour limits ([Yacht Club](https://www.yachtclubgames.com/blog/breaking-the-nes/)), and Toby Fox wanted Undertale to match the Mother games' emotional power, humour and wonder without copying their methods ([Kickstarter/Medium](https://medium.com/kickstarter/undertale-creator-toby-fox-on-the-indie-computer-game-that-s-become-an-industry-darling-fa4df6cee2db)). For a game built on a named predecessor, such as the Digimon World 2-inspired monster raiser the skill will be tried on, the bible should keep an inspiration ledger: what is kept (a feeling or a mechanic), what is changed, what the original never did that this game does, and confirmation that every name, creature concept, character, setting and line of text is original. US-only sources were checked, and patent questions need a qualified reviewer.

### Vision is captured through pillars that can fail content and through specific memories

The best-documented vision tool is the pillar. God of War (2018) was built on three, Combat, Father/Son and Exploration, meant to shape every design decision; its wayward-spirit quests were written around Kratos teaching his son a lesson, and "If the quest didn't serve a definitive purpose, it didn't make it", which cut the first quest built ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2018/12/05/how-santa-monica-studios-nailed-exploration-in-god-of-war/)). The post describes writing to the pillar and cutting what failed it, not rewriting failed quests, so "rewrite toward a pillar before cutting" would be the skill's own policy rather than studio practice. Unpacking's team narrowed a long list of experience adjectives to three pillars, contemplation, discovery and expression, and designed by subtraction, with no scores, fail states or dialogue ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unpacking-the-design-pillars-of-a-chill-puzzle-game)). One studio's narrative guide asks that narrative pillars complement the design pillars and treats tone as a separate field ([Blind Squirrel](https://blindsquirrelentertainment.com/news/Narrative-Design-Part-2/)). A pillar only works as a decision tool if some plausible scene could fail it: "Father/Son" can reject a quest, "fun" cannot. The common "three to five pillars" rule is a blog heuristic. Comparisons need care. One team dropped its "Sunless Sea meets Majora's Mask" pitch after angry comments on Reddit and in the press, and found "inspired by" wording performed about as well ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/on-iterating-your-game-pitch)); the further claim that comps import features the game lacks is opinion ([Indiecator](https://indiecator.org/2023/01/08/on-comparing-games-to-other-games/)). No game-specific method for eliciting an owner's taste was found. The nearest is customer-interview technique: *The Mom Test* treats "I usually", "I would" and "I might" as fluff and redirects to a specific past instance ([mtlynch](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/)), and applying it to creative taste is an inference. Combining these sources gives an intake that asks the owner for two or three tonal references with one specific scene each and what they do *not* want from each; many words for how a session should feel, then three chosen; the game's pillars and which one the story owns; what the player does minute to minute and how it should feel; the moments that must never be undercut; what real place, job, period or experience the game feels like and what the owner knows unusually well; what bothered them about the inspiration and what it never delivered; which traditions are borrowed and who could review them; who the game is not for; and what goes first if a third of the story must be cut. When an answer comes back generic ("epic", "emotional", "like Ghibli"), the follow-up asks for the last time the owner felt it, and in which scene.

## Good premises come from separating generation from judgement

### The first idea is rarely the one to keep, though the slogan is folk

"The first idea is everyone's idea" has no traceable author and should be presented as a folk heuristic ([Wikiquote search](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Ideas)). The closest attributable version is Kevin Kelly's: occasionally the first idea is best, but usually it is about the fifth ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/6003969-over-the-years-i-ve-learned-that-the-first-idea-you)). One plausible mechanism is that first ideas are borrowed, unnoticed, from what the writer has already read or seen ([ProWritingAid](https://prowritingaid.com/art/1238/the-secret-of-good-writing:-idea-generation-and-development.aspx)). The practice of "inverting genre defaults" often credited to Kowal could not be confirmed. What is documented is her filter for choosing among ideas, whether an idea will make a *story* rather than how exciting it is ([Writing Excuses transcripts](https://wetranscripts.dreamwidth.org)), which is effectively the test that turns an idea into a premise: a setting, mechanic or image is not yet a premise until it implies a person who wants something and an obstacle. Three documented generators push past the obvious. Arthur Koestler's bisociation brings two self-consistent but normally unrelated frames of reference into contact ([The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2013/05/20/arthur-koestler-creativity-bisociation/)), though a contemporary review noted that Koestler describes the act without explaining it ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1964/12/17/the-metaphysics-of-arthur-koestler/)). Imposed constraints, from Oulipo's formal games to game-jam themes, are treated in jam research as generative resources, and in a survey of 2013 Global Game Jam participants the theme was the most common source of inspiration ([Riedl et al.](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~riedl/pubs/fdg-ggj13.pdf)). An opposing viewpoint, such as the antagonist's or a rival faction's, is the third. For an agent these become a procedure: generate a wide pool of one-liners, deliberately cut the ones that resemble the genre default or famous works, and give each surviving candidate a different generator so that the options differ in engine, protagonist and player fantasy rather than only in setting. The constraint should be drawn from outside the idea (one location, the protagonist cannot speak, the antagonist is right) rather than chosen to flatter it.

### Loglines detect failure, and owners need help choosing the original option

The professional premise tests are craft heuristics with no evidence that passing them predicts a good story. Blake Snyder's logline asks for irony, a compelling mental picture, audience and cost, and a killer title, with irony the most important ([Save the Cat](https://savethecat.com/?p=35882)), and Randy Ingermanson's one-sentence summary of about 15 words names protagonist, goal, conflict and stakes ([Dabble](https://www.dabblewriter.com/articles/snowflake-method)). Both are good at exposing a premise with no conflict or no hook, and a logline with no built-in contradiction ("a hero must stop a dark lord") is a warning sign. The research evidence concerns who judges ideas well. Justin Berg's study of 339 circus-arts professionals found that creators predicted the success of *other people's* novel ideas better than managers did, but **not of their own**, and that managers undervalued novel ideas in favour of conventional ones ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/managers-are-not-best-judge-creative-ideas)). Rietzschel, Nijstad and Stroebe found people select feasible, desirable ideas over original ones, and that **explicitly instructing selectors to value originality reduced their reluctance to choose original ideas** ([Creativity Research Journal abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400419.2014.901084)). A meta-analysis of 50 choice-overload experiments found a mean effect of about zero ([Scheibehenne et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v37y2010i3p409-425.html)), so "always present three" is folk practice; what matters more is how different the options are and which criteria are stated. Applied to an agent, the evidence says the agent should not present its own ranking as authoritative, should label each option with what it risks and gains, should state originality as a criterion and keep the boldest option in play, and should kill the familiar candidates first while parking near-misses to revisit. Berg's explanation, that judgement improves when the judge has done some divergent thinking first, suggests asking the owner for one or two rough alternatives of their own before showing the agent's; that step extrapolates his account and has not been tested.

### Breaking the story works like a writers' room with the owner as showrunner

In television, "breaking" a story means working out every scene of each storyline. Eric Haywood describes storylines lettered A, B, C and sometimes a "runner", beats competing on a whiteboard so the best idea wins, strands interleaved ("blended"), and act breaks placed as hooks, sometimes first, with the story then worked backwards. The showrunner approves the starting pitch, then hears the broken story pitched back beat by beat until it is approved ([Script Magazine](https://scriptmag.com/features/writers-room-101-beats-breaking-blending)). Jane Espenson moved between whiteboards, index cards and notebook paper and advises using whatever works ([Espenson](https://janeespenson.com/022007/shuffling-the-cards)). Games have borrowed the room. Deck Nine adapted a TV writers' room for Life is Strange: Before the Storm and weighed more perspectives and faster iteration against the risk of compromising the story's voice and vision ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/news/gdc-2018-see-diverse-writers-room-created-life-strange-prequel)). Black River Studios runs short sessions with one goal each (build the universe, define the protagonist, define the villain's threat), sometimes splits participants into opposing groups, bans judgement during capture, and leaves compiling and choosing to the lead ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/leading-a-game-writer-s-room)). Supergiant's Kasavin outlined most of Hades in advance but said designing on paper "can barely mean anything" until a prototype exists ([GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/hades-changes-what-it-means-to-be-a-roguelike/1100-6483420/)). The outlining tools grow in grain. Kenn Adams's Story Spine, an improv tool from the late 1980s that reached Pixar through Rebecca Stockley, gives an eight-line causal skeleton ("once upon a time … every day … but one day … because of that … until finally") ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5750619)). Ingermanson's Snowflake Method expands the one-sentence summary into a paragraph of setup, three disasters and an ending ([Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/snowflake-method)). The TV break gives scene-level detail. The mapping to an agent is direct: the owner is the showrunner who approves the premise and hears the break, and the agent is the room. Strand A becomes the main path, B a companion or relationship arc and C a world or lore runner; blending decides which strand's beats sit in which space; the act break becomes the hook at each chapter, region or run gate. Every beat should be written as what the player does or experiences plus the story change it carries, and marked as fixed (a pillar) or flexible, so that the outline is treated as a prototype for play rather than a contract.

## STORY.md works as a status-marked canon file that consumers cite by ID

### Every documented bible opens with identity and rules

Public series bibles and game narrative documents share a core: a short statement of what the work is and its tone, rules of the world and of the writing, character entries, setting and terminology, and planned arcs. Star Trek: The Next Generation's writers' and directors' guide (final season-one draft, 23 March 1987) opened with the premise, then "The Script" on what is believable and what doesn't work, and it grew from 22 to 53–55 pages across drafts, with character facts changing along the way, such as Troi going from one-quarter to half Betazoid ([BW Media Spotlight](https://bwspotlight.com/2018/07/27/star-trek-the-next-story-bible-introduction)). Ronald D. Moore's December 2003 Battlestar Galactica bible held facts never shown on screen and plans that never happened, such as Tyrol and Baltar as sworn enemies ([The Wertzone](https://thewertzone.blogspot.com/2011/01/battlestar-galactica-series-bible.html)); his "Naturalistic Science Fiction" tone manifesto may be a separate essay to cast and crew rather than the bible's title ([Battlestar Wiki](https://en.battlestarwiki.org/NSF)). A separate "Cylon bible" described the society of a faction the audience never saw from inside, and it was written before the Final Five's identities were decided, so it is an example of a separate world-rules document rather than a sealed answer key ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/first-glimpses-of-bsgs-infamous-cylon-bible-5138376)). Blind Squirrel's 2025 narrative guide starts from a one-sheet (narrative hook, player fantasy, core conflict, main character, antagonist, world, "why now", pillars, tone) and then a lore bible with fields for characters, factions, locations including hazards that affect level design, only important items, world rules, themes and a summary of narrative mechanics. It advises templates, high-level features first, not getting stuck on family trees before plotting, and keeping the bible living until the end of production ([Blind Squirrel Part 2](https://blindsquirrelentertainment.com/news/Narrative-Design-Part-2/); [Part 3](https://blindsquirrelentertainment.com/news/Narrative-Design-Part-3)). Obsidian's Alex Scokel said the Deadfire team kept an internal wiki rather than a fixed design bible, with lore changeable until it ships and "world keepers" able to reject ideas that break how the world works ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/pillars-of-eternity-2/obsidian-interview)). The same interview records Tyranny's writing rules, such as no "kill ten rats" quest, and QA being told to flag NPCs talking down to the player, which makes a short, checkable rules section documented practice. As for whether anyone reads these documents, Ion Hazzikostas said "Most developers on the team have a copy" of World of Warcraft's *Chronicle* books, using the historians only for obscure questions ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/2018/08/14/a-look-inside-how-blizzard-maintains-world-of-warcrafts-lore)). The claim that bibles bloat until nobody reads them appears only on template vendors' blogs ([TaleBuddy](https://talebuddy.io/templates/game-bible)). The usable lesson is a compact canon summary on top with deep reference behind it, and bloat measured by duplication, dead material and content no downstream consumer uses rather than by length.

### Canon control separates recording from deciding

Franchise canon teams document four mechanisms. The first is a status per fact. Lucasfilm's Holocron, a FileMaker database Leland Chee was hired in January 2000 to create, held more than 30,000 entries by 2008 with a canon-level field running from George Lucas's own work down to non-canon, plus reference charts such as which Jedi were alive during the Clone Wars ([Wired mirror](https://wirednews.pages.dev/2008/08/ff-starwarscanon); [StarWars.com](https://www.starwars.com/news/what-is-the-holocron)). The second is reconciliation before retcon: differing Millennium Falcon interiors were explained as Han's modifications, and Chee insisted there is no such thing as fan continuity ([Wired mirror](https://wirednews.pages.dev/2008/08/ff-starwarscanon)). The third is a fenced reset. In 2014 the old Expanded Universe stayed in print "under the new Legends banner" while the films and The Clone Wars became "the immovable objects of Star Wars history" ([StarWars.com](https://www.starwars.com/news/the-legendary-star-wars-expanded-universe-turns-a-new-page)). The fourth is a split between recorder and decider. Game Informer calls Blizzard's historians the definitive authority on what the record says, whose guidance lets others make informed rulings ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/2018/08/14/a-look-inside-how-blizzard-maintains-world-of-warcrafts-lore)), and novelist Christie Golden put it as Sean Copeland tracking what Blizzard decides is canon without making the calls himself ([warcraft.wiki.gg](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sean_Copeland)). When the record conflicts with a plan, the cheaper side changes. Krasus was cut from a Cataclysm raid because he had died in a novel, and Hazzikostas noted that it is easier to patch the game than to reprint a book ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/2018/08/14/a-look-inside-how-blizzard-maintains-world-of-warcrafts-lore)). The same article gives the guardrail: continuity exists to enhance a story, "not to tie the hands of creators". Production adds propagation rules. Rafael Chandler's 2006 voice-asset guide makes one master spreadsheet the authority, edited first, named unmistakably, versioned and owned by one person ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/screen-play-documenting-voice-assets)). The IGDA localisation guide warns that as-recorded changes often reach the game without reaching the script, asks teams to record the reasons for naming decisions, and asks for a date after which source text stops changing ([IGDA PDF](https://igda.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Best-Practices-for-Game-Localization-v22.pdf)). For STORY.md this means one status per entry from a declared legend, each fact stated in one place, the agent as recorder and the owner as decider, an attempt at in-world reconciliation before any retcon, cut material fenced off rather than mixed with live canon, and a changelog stating what changed, why, and which downstream files (VOICES.md, dialogue, quest documents) must be revisited.

### The truth, the tellings and the reveal order belong in separate layers

Mystery-driven and myth-driven stories need three layers that a single narrative cannot hold. The first is the true chronology, including backstory and the antagonist's offscreen moves. The second is the set of in-world versions (myths, rumours, propaganda, lies) labelled with who believes each; Lucasfilm's reconciliation of the "Endor holocaust" as in-universe Imperial propaganda is a documented example of a telling formally distinct from the truth ([Wired mirror](https://wirednews.pages.dev/2008/08/ff-starwarscanon)). The third is the player's learning order, a reveal map in which every must-know conclusion has more than one carrier. Tabletop mystery practice states this as the "Three Clue Rule", known here only from search-indexed text of a well-known design blog ([The Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/tag/running-mysteries)), and it matches the redundancy Jenkins cites. The Case of the Golden Idol's developers tuned their reveal chain by playtest, removing clues that let players skip steps ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/road-to-igf-2023/-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-i-used-frequent-testing-to-improve-its-mystery-solving)). A matrix of character against key fact against stage (knows, suspects, believes the myth, doesn't know) is the structure behind stage-dependent NPC lines, and it answers the question game-dialogue asks before every line, what the speaker and player know at this moment. No published studio example of such a matrix was found, so it is an inference. Staff documents should not be spoiler-tiered. The IGDA guide wants translators to have played or read the content first, at least three days' play plus background documents for a small title, and Damien Yoccoz asks for deeper character backstories than players ever see ([IGDA PDF](https://igda.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Best-Practices-for-Game-Localization-v22.pdf); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/how-to-create-a-perfect-localization-kit)). A spoiler-free blurb is needed only for public use.

### Handoff fields come from voice and localisation practice

Voice and localisation are the best-documented consumers of story documents. Chandler's per-line fields include character, cue, context, trigger, inflection, location and filename, with different column sets for actors, the director and designers ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/screen-play-documenting-voice-assets)). The IGDA guide asks for chronological script order, a note of whom each line addresses, and a casting package of age, sex, voice type, background, a similar-voice reference and an image, and it wants voice described precisely (period, region, register) rather than "sounds like Brad Pitt" ([IGDA PDF](https://igda.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Best-Practices-for-Game-Localization-v22.pdf)). Yoccoz's localisation kit adds target player profile, tone, full character introductions in which gender is essential for gendered languages, world details, context hints and strings ordered chronologically, never alphabetically ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/how-to-create-a-perfect-localization-kit)). Game-dialogue already treats a project's VOICES.md as binding and reads canon from the narrative documents, so the clean split is that STORY.md holds the canon half of these fields (age, gender and pronouns, role, relationships, background, secrets, forms of address, key or secondary status) and points to VOICES.md for how each character sounds. The table below assembles a section list from the sources in this section. It is a synthesis; no studio publishes a game story-bible template.

## Genre sets the story load, and acclaimed entries tie story to the loop

### Story appetite varies by player, and blocking play fails in every genre

No public dataset ranks genres by how much story players want. Quantic Foundry's motivation model is the best evidence. Story is one of 12 motivations, defined as elaborate plots and interesting characters, sitting with Fantasy (which holds deep lore) in an Immersion cluster, and players who score low on it prefer open-ended games with no overarching narrative and skip dialogue and quest text where they can ([QF reference PDF](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Gamer-Motivation-Model-Reference.pdf)). In shooters, Competition versus Story is the main axis separating audiences, and the two are strongly negatively correlated ([QF shooter map](https://quanticfoundry.com/2016/06/22/shooter-genre-map/), via search-indexed text). The practical rule is that critical information never lives only in skippable text, because a large share of players will skip it. Cheung, Zimmermann and Nagappan's study of first-hour reviews, drawn from 212 Amazon reviews and 35 long-form reviews of mostly shooter and action Xbox 360 games plus six industry interviews, calls the first hour a "probationary period" ([Cheung et al., CHI PLAY 2014](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/First20Hour20-20CHIPlay20201420-20preprint2.pdf)). A good narrative acts as a "holdout" that keeps players going despite frustration, players stick around for anticipated elements such as a first dragon, and the paper's headline implication is that intrigue trumps enjoyment: cues about depth still to come carry players further than momentary fun. One reviewer quit Blitz: The League over cutscenes he thought were unskippable, and the paper reveals they could in fact be skipped, so the rule is that skipping must be possible *and obvious*. Within every genre, acclaimed games span the whole range of story load. Dead Cells' lead designer wanted little or no story because lore slowed the loop between death and the next run, and the narrative document Motion Twin has now was patched together after the fact in half French and half English ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2019-04-03-dead-cells-story-lore-interview-gdc-2019.html)). Hades runs to about 305,000 words, more than two-thirds of them dialogue, with roughly 21,000 voice lines (counts vary with what is counted), and Hades II to more than 400,000 words and 30,000 lines ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2020/12/supergiant_shares_hades_voiceover_stats_charon_hardly_gets_a_word_in); [Cultured Vultures](https://culturedvultures.com/?p=142587); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/hades/hades-2-has-an-epic-script-of-over-400-000-words-and-30-000-voice-lines-around-50-percent-more-than-the-original-roguelike)). Supergiant treats a difficulty wall that strands story-minded players as a failure ([GDC Podcast](https://gdconf.com/news/roguelikes-and-narrative-design-hades-creative-director-greg-kasavin-gdc-podcast-ep-16)), which is why Hades has its God Mode ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/hades-god-mode-interview)). The table below is a synthesis of how much bible each genre needs, not a measured ranking.

| Genre | Story load the bible should carry | Documented failure to check for |
|---|---|---|
| Puzzle, casual | Premise, tone, a light frame | Story that interrupts the puzzle loop |
| Platformer, metroidvania | Theme carried by mechanics and level hazards; lore in the environment | Expository dumps; story fighting play |
| Action, shooter | Clear motive and stakes, kept skippable | Unskippable or non-obviously skippable scenes |
| Roguelite | A reason to return after each death, short hub beats, a large line pool | Repeated lines after a few deaths; story behind a skill wall |
| Action RPG with procedural floors | Premise, lore that escalates with depth, hub characters who react to progress | Every floor feeling the same narratively |
| Cozy, life sim | Relationship arcs unlocked over time; open ending for the protagonist only | Shallow villagers; dangling threads players invested in |
| JRPG, creature collector | A full bible: cast, arcs, antagonist thesis, creature mystery | The generic ladder-rival-evil-team template saying nothing |
| Horror | Place, wrongness and a late summoning | Dread built on jump scares |

### Roguelites make death the delivery mechanism, or keep story to fragments

Two documented approaches work for run-based games. In the maximal one, death advances the story. Kasavin's stated early goal for Hades was "to take the pain out of dying and having to restart", so narrative progress persists across deaths the way gear does elsewhere ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)). He reasoned that because death is not final in a roguelike, the protagonist and the bosses can remember earlier runs ([GDC Podcast](https://gdconf.com/news/roguelikes-and-narrative-design-hades-creative-director-greg-kasavin-gdc-podcast-ep-16)), and because the game can be played indefinitely, players should not hit repeated dialogue soon ([Cultured Vultures](https://culturedvultures.com/?p=142587)). The ending is layered: credits roll on the tenth escape, and a true epilogue sits behind further relationship requirements ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/121621/how-to-unlock-the-epilogue-hades); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Hades/walkthrough/9)). Probabilistic gating has a cost. One Hades II player unlocked three major side stories only after about 105 runs and had assumed they were bugged ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/hades/after-105-runs-in-hades-2-i-suddenly-unlocked-three-huge-side-stories-i-thought-were-bugged-and-i-guess-ill-be-here-til-launch)), so content gated behind many runs needs a visible sign that more exists. In the minimal approach (Dead Cells, Slay the Spire) story stays in fragments and out of the loop. Darkest Dungeon sits between, with a single narrator who reacts to outcomes and systems that generate story through stress and affliction ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-darkest-dungeon-i-designing-for-despair-and-kicking-you-when-you-re-down)). Returnal's narrative director said its time loop let the team hide layers that players uncover through repetition, and a senior narrative designer called balancing clarity against mystery a tightrope ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2021/02/25/returnal-ps5-housemarque-interview/); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/returnals-mystery-was-a-tightrope-walk-says-senior-narrative-designer-3251502)). For a procedural descent, such as the 100-floor Balkan folk-horror action RPG the skill will be tried on, these sources suggest hub characters who react to the deepest floor reached, the last cause of death and the number of returns, with events gated on state and weighted; lore fragments keyed to depth bands so revelations escalate; authored beats on fixed milestone floors between generated ones; and an ending in layers. The milestone-floor pattern is inferred from Hades' fixed bosses rather than described by any developer in those terms.

### Creature collectors are panned for the template only when it says nothing

Critics pan the default creature-collector template (badge ladder, rival, evil team, legendary creature) when it is executed generically. Temtem's story was called "cookie cutter" ([MP1st](https://mp1st.com/reviews/temtem-review-gotta-card-em-all-ps5)), Coromon's flat and a major weakness ([Goomba Stomp](https://goombastomp.com/coromon-review/)), and Digimon World 2 sits at a Metacritic 42, though from only five critic reviews, with complaints aimed at slow pacing and repetition ([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/digimon-world-2/)). The praised entries give the story an argument about creatures and people, or build the bond into a mechanic. Kotaku files Pokémon Black and White's liberation philosophy under the game's best part while noting that the story stops short of a more radical conclusion and absorbs N's beliefs into another villain's scheme ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/pokemon-games-best-worst-part-scarlet-violet-red-blue-1850084491/6)). In Cassette Beasts the relationship with a human partner unlocks and scales fusion power, so the bond is literally a combat stat ([Analog Stick Gaming](https://www.analogstickgaming.com/game-reviews/2023/4/23/cassette-beasts); [Cassette Beasts wiki](https://wiki.cassettebeasts.com/wiki/Fusion_Power)). Monster Hunter Stories 2 is about the responsibility of Ratha's power, which is neither good nor bad ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/monster-hunter-stories-2-wings-of-ruin/monster-hunter-stories-2-wings-of-ruin-review-my)). Shin Megami Tensei's demon negotiation came from a developer's frustration that Dungeons & Dragons goblins were only loot, not beings to talk to ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/megami-tensei-developers-frustration-with-goblin-encounters-in-dungeons-dragons-is-what-inspired-the-series-demon-negotiation-system/)). Digimon World 1's feral, memory-less Digimon regain their memory when recruited and rebuild File City, so recruiting is the story ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digimon_World)). No developer talk on creature-collector narrative design was found, and the idea that evolution or fusion carries theme is critics' and fans' interpretation. The checks that follow from the reception are whether the antagonist holds an argument about what people owe the creatures they use and whether the game takes that argument seriously, whether the creatures' own nature is a mystery revealed in stages, whether at least one roster mechanic carries the bond theme with a story consequence, whether the ladder, rival and evil team each do thematic work, and whether the hub visibly changes as the story advances.

### Folk horror runs on landscape, isolation and belief that is logical from inside

Adam Scovell's "folk horror chain", as described in academic sources, runs from a landscape that harms the identity of the people living there, to isolation, to skewed belief and morality, to a happening or summoning. James Thurgill argues the chain becomes problematic when rurality is treated as the same thing as landscape ([Revenant](https://www.revenantjournal.com/contents/a-fear-of-the-folk-on-topophobia-and-the-horror-of-rural-landscapes/); [Little Toller](https://www.littletoller.co.uk/the-clearing/poetry/can-folk-horror-tell-us-landscape-adam-scovell/)). One critic offers a games-ready test, that folk horror is when the land is hungry, with complicit insiders asking who will kill or die rather than whether the ritual is wrong ([Rascal](https://www.rascal.news/i-was-sacrificed-for-the-land-and-all-i-got-was-this-essay/)). Mundaun shows cultural specificity carrying a game: a real valley its developer has visited since childhood, hand-pencilled textures, and voice acting entirely in Romansh ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/119313/mundauns-creator-discusses-the-folklore-pencil-art-and-more); [Swiss Revue](https://www.swisscommunity.org/en/news-media/swiss-revue/article/handcrafted-swiss-horror)). Its developer says some characters are Swiss folklore regulars and others were invented during development ([Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/video-games/3648922/interview-mundaun-developer-michel-ziegler-discusses-folk-horror-real-life-experiences-behind-pencil-drawn-horror-game/), via search-indexed text). Morteshka's documented method for One-Eyed Likho is to take a specific tale from a published 19th-century collection and change it deliberately, for example turning a tailor who is quickly disposed of in the original into a surviving companion ([Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-one-eyed-likho-brings-slavic-folk-horror-to-life), via search-indexed text). Brian Gomez, design director at Vatra Games, recalls telling the Silent Hill: Downpour team in Brno to stop trying to be "Japanese scary" and just be "Czech scary" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/survival-horror/silent-hill-downpour-devs-reflect-12-years-on-stop-trying-to-be-japanese-scary-and-just-be-czech-scary)). Pathologic 2's developers aim for constant low-level dread rather than jump scares ([Game Tyrant](https://gametyrantindustries.squarespace.com/news/interview-pathologic-2-will-bring-horror-through-omnipresent-dread-instead-of-jump-scares)), and an academic reading of Blasphemous treats its cycles of death and resurrection as penance built into the mechanics ([Acta Ludologica](https://actaludologica.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/AL_2025-8-2_Study-6_Varini-Costalunga.pdf)). Balkan-folklore games so far are small indies without critical reception, and no source names which South Slavic ethnographic collections suit a game, so that choice needs research and, ideally, a tradition-bearer to review it. The checks for a folk-horror bible are whether the premise passes the chain, what the land is hungry for and who pays, whether each folklore element is traced to a named source with its deliberate changes recorded, whether the horror is regional in language, ritual and landscape rather than imported, whether the villagers' beliefs are logical from inside, and whether dread comes from place, guilt and system. Darkest Dungeon's rationed triumphs and reversals are the best-documented defence against misery fatigue across a long dark game.

## Quest design needs beats stated as world state, not as scenes

### A quest is an NPC motivation made of actions with preconditions

The best-evidenced structural model of quests is Doran and Parberry's analysis of **753 quests** from EverQuest, World of Warcraft, Eve Online and Vanguard. Every quest starts from one of nine NPC motivations (Conquest 20.2%, Equipment 18.5%, Knowledge 18.3%, Protection 18.2%, Serenity 13.7%, Reputation 6.5%, Wealth 2.0%, Comfort 1.6%, Ability 1.1%), which the NPC pursues through two to seven strategies per motivation, each a verb-noun pair made of one to six actions ([Doran & Parberry, PCG 2011](https://ianparberry.com/pubs/pcg2011.pdf)). The paper defines 20 atomic actions, each with a precondition and a postcondition; `give`, for instance, requires that somebody is there and you have something, and leaves them having it and you not. The authors found that human authors produce significantly less structural variety than expected, changing setting while keeping the relationships between actions, and they argue a generator may elaborate a quest or insert subquests freely as long as each pattern's pre- and postconditions hold. The nine categories are NPC concerns, not "quest types", as some summaries call them. Yu, Guzdial and Sturtevant interviewed 15 developers from BioWare, Bungie, Ubisoft and eight indie studios and found no consensus definition of a quest; seven of the fifteen created or changed a definition during the interview, and the developers spoke more about what quests are *for* (guiding the player, telling the narrative, teaching the rules) than what they are ([arXiv 2110.04148](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.04148)). Their Definition–Context–Purpose framing suits a handoff: each mission states its purposes, and a mission whose only purpose is "content" is a cut candidate. The familiar "hook, complication, resolution, reward" anatomy has no canonical primary source and is folk vocabulary.

### Acclaimed studios translate a beat's core idea into play

CD Projekt Red's process is the best documented. Writers usually seed main quests and quest designers many side quests; a chosen pitch becomes a full *scenario* covering the underlying events, the key scenes and what happens where, followed by several passes, an asset list, a placeholder build and a mood-polish pass ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/94509/witcher-3-quest-designers-talk-design-process-most-difficult-quest-to-create)). Mateusz Tomaszkiewicz said the team "declared war" on fetch quests, keeping an item request only if it led to a meaningful conversation, choice or secret ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/the-witcher-3-quest-designers-talk-balancing-story-with-gameplay-and-declaring-war-on-fetch-quests/)). Paweł Sasko's GDC 2023 lessons add: withhold key information so players want the story, put emotion over set pieces, cut repeated exposition, keep critical information out of traversal chatter, set up stakes before a choice and offer the options a player and protagonist would logically expect, and make consequences delayed but visible, since hidden ones are not worth the investment ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/10-key-takeaways-from-the-quest-design-of-cyberpunk-2077-and-the-witcher-3)). The Bloody Baron's first script ran about 40 pages before any dialogue ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/the-witcher/the-first-script-for-the-witcher-3s-best-quest-was-40-pages-long-before-a-single-line-of-dialog-was-even-added)), and in September 2026 Sasko said he made many mistakes in it: at almost four hours it should have been self-contained blocks of about 40 minutes of theme-focused play, each followed by a breather, with finales the exception ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/a-witcher-3-dev-reflects-on-its-most-memorable-quest-the-bloody-baron-quest-i-made-so-many-mistakes-2000730612)). That figure is one designer's working practice, not a standard. Obsidian audits solution coverage: Josh Sawyer describes an informal "Karma Police" job counting how often each skill is checked across scripts so every skill gets enough cool things to do ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/fallout/fallout-new-vegas-director-josh-sawyer-says-obsidian-has-karma-police-who-go-through-the-game-making-sure-every-skill-gets-enough-cool-stuff-to-do-were-not-gonna-leave-you-out-to-dry/)), and leads ask quest and area designers early which skills can solve each problem, with every taggable skill given a use in the starting town ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/rpg/fallout-new-vegas-director-on-the-art-of-making-sure-all-your-points-in-persuasion-arent-wasted-its-all-about-feelings-but-you-use-numbers-to-arrive-at-that-feeling)). Bethesda's Bruce Nesmith said players wanted only so many generated job quests that did not advance the story, and that using Radiant for the main quest was rejected because a hand-crafted line is more compelling ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/bethesdas-nesmith-reflects-on-the-difficult-birth-of-skyrims-radiant-story-system/)). Casey Hudson agreed that in Mass Effect 2 team-building *is* the story ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010/06/15/interview-bioware-casey-hudson-on-the-making-of-mass-effect-2/)). Ubisoft's Julien Charpentier estimates about one hour of story per ten hours of open-world play, and The Division's narrative designers asked level designers for enemy-free spaces where conversations could play ([Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/7m412GLSbfkaT0YheRYLVG/what-is-narrative-design)). The handoff boundary that follows is CDPR's scenario stage. The story skill delivers underlying events, key scenes, what happens where and the emotional target of each beat; quest design takes over verbs, solutions, assets, triggers and scripting; dialogue takes the lines.

### Consequences need a setter, a carrier, a reader and a signal

Reactivity is budgeted by triage, and fallbacks for plot-critical content are mandatory. Larian's "n+1" rule, dating from Original Sin, assumes the player may kill every antagonist and protagonist, so plot-critical content always has another route; Swen Vincke has said one MacGuffin had roughly two dozen delivery routes at one point, a count he hedged ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/baldurs-gate/baldurs-gate-3-has-at-least-24-ways-shadowheart-can-hurl-the-main-macguffin-at-you-as-part-of-larians-new-vegas-style-n1-system-for-plot-oblivious-players-it-might-be-even-more)). Scripter Mihail Kostov described a mandatory edge-case checklist covering NPCs killed or knocked out, items stolen or destroyed, dialogue failing to start or being interrupted, party members in different places and fights that merge ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/rpg/baldurs-gate-3-programmer-says-its-larians-philosophy-to-fuel-and-reward-player-creativity-they-found-a-way-to-exploit-the-game-let-them-have-it-its-awesome)). A fallback can cost too little. New Vegas lead writer John Gonzalez thinks the Yes Man route may have been a mistake because it lets the player finish without getting their hands dirty ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fallout/fallout-new-vegas-lead-writer-loved-writing-yes-man-but-thinks-his-questline-may-have-been-a-mistake-it-lets-you-get-through-the-game-without-getting-your-hands-dirty)), and Sawyer called the allocation of ending slides "mostly arbitrary", decided by what the team found compelling ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fallout/a-minor-fallout-new-vegas-quest-got-its-own-ending-slide-because-the-team-was-transfixed-by-the-choice-where-you-make-the-ncrs-worst-soldiers-take-psycho/)). Promised consequences that fade are the documented failure: critics found Cyberpunk 2077's lifepaths mattered little beyond the opening ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/cyberpunk-2077-life-paths-pointless/)), narrative director Philipp Weber called them something the team would like to improve ([GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/articles/life-paths-may-have-a-bigger-impact-on-story-in-cyberpunk-2/)), and Sasko called the linear-story criticism justified ([Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/news/2023/01/cyberpunk-2077-dev-acknowledges-linear-story-criticism-says-its-justified)). Free-order and generated content keep a fixed story intact in three documented ways. Hades serves beats when their conditions are met; ladder games put story on gates (Monster Hunter's Urgent Quests, Pokémon's eight badges); and Breath of the Wild makes its reveals past-tense memories that can be found in any order, though the final memory unlocks only after all the others ([Moreno, GDC 2018 Game Narrative Review, a student analysis](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/GameNarrativeReview/Moreno_NarrativeReview.pdf)). FromSoftware's order-dependent NPC questlines, patched with map markers after Elden Ring's launch, are the counter-example ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/elden-rings-npc-quest-markers-are-just-a-band-aid/)). Four checkable parts follow for every consequence: the flag is *set* when the choice resolves, *carried* through saves and act breaks, *read* by a named later beat, and *signalled* so the player can attribute the payoff. Reactivity can be budgeted in tiers: fallbacks that must work, cheap acknowledgements, local variants and rare structural branches announced to production early. The tiers are an inference consistent with Larian, Obsidian and CDPR, not a published scheme. No studio has published flag counts or costs per branch.

### What the beat map hands to quest design

No studio publishes a beat-sheet or quest one-pager template. The fields below are a synthesis of what the documented processes need as input: CDPR's scenario contents, Sasko's rules, Obsidian's solution plan, Larian's fallbacks and Doran and Parberry's preconditions.

| Field | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Beat ID | Stable ID with act or chapter | Flags, missions, dialogue and voice files cite it |
| Core idea and emotional target | One sentence the beat must keep when made playable; what the player should think and feel at the end | CDPR co-designs the core idea, then translates it into play |
| Delivery mode | Do, find, choose or watch, with a reason for any "watch" | Sasko and Tomaszkiewicz on gameplay-free stretches |
| Facts revealed and withheld | What the player learns; what must stay unknown until later | Sasko's withholding; leak checks across free-order paths |
| Preconditions | World-state flags required, never "after mission N" | Doran & Parberry; Hades eligibility |
| Postconditions | Flags written, who lives or dies, faction changes, items | Consequence carry |
| Choice | Options as expected moves, stakes established beforehand, flags each writes | Sasko on stakes and logical options |
| Payoff beats | Which later beats read each flag, and how the payoff is signalled | Delayed but visible consequences |
| Criticality and fallback | Whether the beat is plot-critical; its n+1 route if the NPC dies, the item is lost or the region is skipped | Larian n+1; Kostov's checklist; Yes Man's warning on costless fallbacks |
| Theme link | The theme the beat states, echoes or counterpoints | The Bloody Baron's two fathers who lost daughters |
| Gate | A progression gate (boss, rank, badge, hub return, fixed floor) or free order | Monster Hunter, Pokémon, Hades |
| Purposes (per mission) | Story beat, teach a mechanic, introduce a character, breather | Yu et al.'s Definition–Context–Purpose |
| Quest-giver motivation and protagonist stake (per mission) | A Doran & Parberry motivation and strategy, plus why the player character cares | Avoids the MMO pattern of a giver's want with no personal stake |
| Solution space (per mission) | Intended verbs or skills, left open for quest design | Obsidian's coverage audit |

## Auditing starts from the as-built story and defaults to revision

### Diagnosis compresses each unit to the change it makes

The professional diagnosis methods share one move: before judging, compress each unit of the existing story into one line saying what it does and what changes, then test those lines against a few global questions. Story Grid's primer is the most concrete version. Each unit gets a Story Event (one sentence naming the action, the tactic and the value that changes) and is tested against Five Commandments: an inciting incident, a turning-point progressive complication (the primer's "phere", an unexpected event that turns the value), a crisis posed as an either/or dilemma, a climax in which a character decides and acts, and a resolution. The Editor's Six Core Questions cover genre, conventions and obligatory events, point of view, objects of desire (including the antagonist's), the controlling idea, and a beginning hook, middle build and ending payoff that each carry their own commandments. The spreadsheet records per scene the Story Event, value shift, turning point, point of view, duration, location and onstage and offstage characters ([Story Grid 101](https://storygrid.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/STORY-GRID-101-Print.pdf)). Writing centres teach the reverse outline, built after a draft exists with fields for what each unit does and what it needs, and done before changing anything so structure surfaces before line work is wasted ([Miami University](https://miamioh.edu/howe-center/hwc/writing-resources/handouts/writing-process/reverse-outlining.html); [CCNY](https://www.ccny.cuny.edu/sites/default/files/writing/upload/ReverseOutline%E2%80%93ARevisionTool.pdf)). Script coverage supplies a report format: logline, synopsis, a grid rating premise, characters, plot and dialogue from poor to excellent, and a verdict of pass, consider or recommend, with readers weighting execution over concept ([Wikipedia: Script coverage](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Script_reader)). Pixar's Braintrust supplies the social rules. Ed Catmull concluded that "the Braintrust should have no authority" so a director never feels undermined, and he and John Lasseter told Disney's directors they need not follow anyone's notes, theirs included ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/spark-creativity-with-these-tips-from-pixars-president)). No study shows that any of these methods improves revisions; they are craft tradition.

### Each recurring problem has a mechanical check

Most named story problems reduce to the per-unit test. A passive protagonist shows up as act climaxes where someone else makes the deciding choice, which is exactly what Hades II's Patch 1 fixed. A convenient escape fails Coats's coincidence rule. The "idiot plot", a plot that moves only because everyone behaves like an idiot, is a term Damon Knight suggested may have originated with James Blish and that Roger Ebert popularised in 1985 ([Wikipedia: Idiot plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiot_plot)); its check is whether one obvious question or disclosure would dissolve an obstacle, and if so, whether the character has a stated reason not to ask or tell. Exposition problems have Supergiant's Bastion rules as a reference: reveal backstory gradually and in context, don't expect players to care about things not on screen, add information the player could not otherwise know rather than describing what is visible, and keep lines short ([Supergiant](https://supergiantgames.com/blog/in-depth-writing-bastion)). Portal's playtests showed the puzzle game needed narrative to hold players past about 15 to 30 minutes, and the answer was short announcements per test chamber rather than front-loaded exposition ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/feature-thinking-with-portals-creating-valve-s-new-ip-)). Character load is countable from the spreadsheet, and Destiny's reboot merged characters (the Crow became Prince Uldren, Osiris's assistant became the Stranger) rather than keeping them all ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-messy-true-story-behind-the-making-of-destiny-1737556731)). One problem is specific to games. Anthem's scenes were often performance-captured once, so when gameplay changed, lines stopped fitting: characters refer to a javelin being destroyed in a mission where it never is, and talk about others as absent while they stand in the room ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964)). A fact table per line, diffed against the new behaviour whenever gameplay changes, would catch it. "Sagging middle", "theme sermon" and "tonal whiplash" are folk labels with no authoritative definition or threshold; the closest sourced tool for a sagging middle is Story Grid's requirement that the middle build carry its own crisis and climax.

### Revision beats a late tear-down in every documented case

The documented production rewrites favour targeted revision over clean slates, though every case is reported journalism or developer recollection and the sample is biased toward troubled projects. In July 2013 Destiny's writers showed leadership a roughly two-hour supercut of cinematics and major beats; it was judged too campy and linear and scrapped, a small group sketched a new plot in about two weeks of "Iron Bar" meetings, existing missions were cut apart and recombined into what one source called a "Franken-story", and the game shipped in September 2014 at a Metacritic of 76 against internal projections of about 90 ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-messy-true-story-behind-the-making-of-destiny-1737556731)). Anthem's real production happened in its last 18 months, partly because of narrative reboots; David Gaider's departure in early 2016 triggered a total story reboot, the story, core loop and missions were largely made in the final 12 to 16 months, and the game launched at 55 after mock reviews predicted the high 70s ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964)). Portal 2's writers began with a concept that dropped portals, Chell and GLaDOS, and playtesters told them it wasn't Portal 2, so all three came back ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2012-03-08-the-importance-of-playtesting-what-portal-2-could-have-been.html)). The successful post-launch fixes added content and moved agency while keeping the spine: Mass Effect 3's free Extended Cut, Final Fantasy XV's patched Chapter 13 scenes ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/final-fantasy-15-update-covers-chapter-13-changes-/1100-6448979)), and Hades II's Patch 1. Halo 2 cut practically its entire third act and ended on a cliffhanger, according to developer interviews collected by a fan wiki ([Halopedia](https://halopedia.org/Earth_Ark)), which shows the exposure of a story whose payoff sits in the last missions. "Murder your darlings" does not license structural demolition either. It comes from Arthur Quiller-Couch's 1914 Cambridge lecture "On Style", was aimed at self-consciously fine writing, and was attached to Faulkner later ([Slate](https://www.slate.com/blogs/browbeat/2013/10/18/_kill_your_darlings_writing_advice_what_writer_really_said_to_murder_your.html)). The audit rules that follow: recommend replacing the spine only when the global want, controlling idea or ending cannot be made coherent with existing content, and then price the content cost (missions reordered, voice re-recorded, cinematics re-shot). Present fixes Braintrust-style, as two or three options each with its trade-off and the units it touches, and let the owner choose. Record the elements the owner loves as protected before proposing changes, as Portal 2's testers revealed what had to stay. Prefer additive fixes for shipped or locked content. Mark load-bearing and modular beats so that every act pays something off on its own.

### A story that lives in data is audited as a graph

When a story is scattered across scene scripts, mission tables and flags, it can be treated as a directed graph of units with preconditions and effects. Jon Manning of Secret Lab, the Yarn Spinner developers, showed at GDC 2019 how symbolic execution and control-flow analysis can prove a dialogue branch unreachable ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025981/Compiling-Your-Story-Using-Techniques)). ChoiceScript's random playthroughs and graph traversal find unreachable passages ([IF craft corpus](https://pvliesdonk.github.io/if-craft-corpus/corpus-docs/craft-foundations/testing_interactive_fiction)). An academic validator checks a Twine export with depth-first search ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/5472960/files/Validating%20the%20plot%20of%20interactive%20Narrative%20games.pdf)). Obsidian's Avowed QA lead described moving from pass/fail bug hunting to joint QA, design and narrative analysis of branching content while scenes and flags could still change ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/2878-avowed-branching-narratives-shifting-from-qa-testing-to-analyzing-clip/), via search-indexed text). None of these tools judges tone or pacing. No public write-up describes reconstructing a large commercial game's story from its data, so the procedure that follows is a synthesis. Inventory every story-bearing source (design docs, scene scripts, cutscene lists, mission and quest tables, dialogue databases, item and lore text, flag definitions). Normalise each unit to a row with its ID and source, type, trigger and preconditions, effects, characters present or mentioned, facts asserted, Story Event and value shift, and change cost (voiced or cinematic). Build the graph and check for unreachable units, flags read but never set or set but never read, reveals that can fire before their setup on some path, dead-end states and contradictory assertions. Reconstruct the canonical path and major variants as a scene sheet, separate the as-built story from the as-intended one, and only then run the craft checks. The cautionary case for doing this at all is Blizzard, where a Diablo historian reportedly had to hand-transcribe every spoken line of the first two games because nobody had kept track ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/an-official-diablo-lore-historian-once-had-to-hand-transcribe-all-of-the-words-spoken-in-the-first-two-games-because-blizzard-hadnt-kept-track-of-them), headline-level).

### Narrative playtests ask players to tell the story back

Studio user-research groups have presented methods for testing story early, before content locks. Microsoft's Deborah Hendersen described "narrative usability" work aimed at finding twists that don't land and confusing conclusions, and at tracking how players' perceptions of characters change over time ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020526/Using-User-Research-to-Improve)). Ubisoft's Jonathan Dankoff presented a narrative review process run early enough for writers to act, motivated by playtesters normally reaching the story too late ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022166)). Their instruments are not public. Academic work proposes analysing the stories players tell about their play as less leading than direct questions ([Kreminski et al.](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/EvaluatingViaRetellings)). Naughty Dog's playtests of The Last of Us Part II showed players disliking characters meant to be likable, which led to revisions and added scope ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/as-naughty-dog-crunches-on-the-last-of-us-ii-developer-1842289962)). Unity exposes a standard cutscene-skip analytics event ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/2018.1/Documentation/ScriptReference/Analytics.AnalyticsEvent.CutsceneSkip.html)), but no studio has published skip-rate benchmarks, so the useful reading is which scenes are skipped and where in the scene. A revised story should ship with a test plan: a retell scored against the STORY.md beat list, perception questions per named character, expectation questions before and after each reveal, and per-scene skip telemetry.

## Language models fail at the structural choices the craft protects

### Model stories are low in tension, friendly and alike

The empirical record on model-written stories is consistent across model families and generations, and it maps onto the story layer as closely as the dialogue report's evidence maps onto lines. Tian and colleagues paired 819 film synopses with GPT-4 versions written from the same premises. Over half of the GPT-4 stories followed a man-in-a-hole arc, tragic arcs nearly vanished (riches to rags fell from 14.6% of human stories to 1.3%, Oedipus from 9.3% to 1.7%), and the major setback and climax arrived *early*, leaving the rest flat. The models were also poor readers of structure: humans identified turning points with 46.6% accuracy against 18.8–31.3% for the models tested ([Tian et al., EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13248v2)). A 2026 preprint measuring tension as how often a model fails to predict a story's continuation found stories from frontier models, including Claude Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.2, the least tense of any corpus tested, below amateur human writing: a late-stage miss rate of 0.215 against 0.607 for New Yorker fiction, and a median of **22% of tension retained after a peak against 54%** ([Spoiler Alert](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09854v1)). The same stories scored *above* the New Yorker on the EQ-Bench rubric. Model casts are friendlier than human ones. In about 1,200 science-fiction stories from four models, the average relationship sign ran from roughly +0.24 to +0.66 against −0.061 for public-domain human fiction, and negative ties clustered far less ([Nonaka & Perry](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18932), a workshop preprint whose exact values the fact-check advises confirming against the PDF). Rettberg and Wigers generated 11,800 stories from gpt-4o-mini, 50 for each of 236 countries, and found one dominant plot: a protagonist in or returning to a small town meets a minor threat and resolves it by organising a community event such as a festival, favouring nostalgia and stability over change. Twenty-three of 50 American titles began "The Last Train", and 33 of 50 Norwegian titles used "Whispering" ([Open Research Europe](https://ore.eu/legacy/articles/5-202), under review). Convergence crosses vendors. Of 20,000 stories from Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, GPT-5.4-Mini and OLMo given minimal prompts, **88.3% contained at least one of 11 recurring tokens**; "lighthouse" appeared in 51.2%, Elias in 26.5%, Mara in 16.7% and Elara in 13.1%, and 56% of Claude's stories were titled "The Lighthouse Keeper's Secret" ([Hamilton & Mimno, EMNLP 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26492)). Antislop's frequency analysis found "Elara" 85,513 times more common in gemma-3-12b output than in human text ([Antislop, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15061)). Different models give strikingly similar answers to open-ended prompts ([Artificial Hivemind, NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954)), and on average models produce fewer than four meaningfully distinct responses in ten samples ([NoveltyBench, COLM 2025](https://novelty-bench.github.io)). AI help raises individual quality while narrowing the pool: writers given GPT-4 ideas produced stories rated more creative and better written, especially the less creative writers, but the stories were more similar to each other ([Doshi & Hauser, *Science Advances*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38996021/)). Professional co-writing studies report the same thing qualitatively. Wordcraft's 13 writers found a bland default voice, a pull toward warrior-hero tropes, reluctance to write people doing mean things, and a character named "Sarah" inserted into every participant's story because it appeared in the prompt's examples ([Wordcraft](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.05030)). Dramatron's 15 professionals found output too literal and predictable, with incomplete character journeys and no subtext, and valued only its log-line-to-scene hierarchy ([Dramatron](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.14958)). Values can run the other way from the stereotype: GPT-4 stories were more progressive than human ones on gender roles, while their scenarios were less imaginative ([Beguš](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12902)). Expert pass rates, the LAMP edit taxonomy and soft villainy in role-play are covered in the dialogue report.

### The defaults are trained in, so newer models will not remove them

The mechanism points to training rather than to any one model. Kirk and colleagues found that RLHF generalises better than supervised fine-tuning but significantly reduces output diversity ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452)). The verbalized-sampling authors locate the main cause in "typicality bias" in preference data, where annotators favour familiar text ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)). Hamilton and Mimno found their recurring tokens rare in published literature but several times denser in alignment datasets than in real users' story requests ([Hamilton & Mimno](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26492)). Because homogeneity holds across vendors, switching or ensembling models will not by itself remove the defaults, and the persistence from GPT-4 in 2023 to 2026 frontier models suggests guardrails should assume the failures are present. Generating more does not escape them either. Of 4,000 model-generated research seed ideas, only 200 were non-duplicates at a 0.8 similarity threshold ([Si, Yang & Hashimoto, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109)), so variety has to come from varied inputs (constraints, reference works, arc types), not from a larger sample. Two caveats bound all of this. No study examines story-level model failures in game narratives (quest structures, branching, lore bibles), so the transfer is inference, though a well-supported one. And no corpus counts exist for the fantasy defaults that circulate in practitioner lists (Lyra, Seraphina, Thorne, Eldoria, chosen ones, prophecies, amnesiac heroes), which should be soft flags rather than bans.

### Models cannot certify their own stories

Self-review is compromised in three documented ways. Assistants gave more positive feedback on arguments and poems when the user said "I wrote" or "I really like" the text ([Sharma et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13548v4)), so an audit framed as "this is my game's story" will soften. GPT-4 and Llama 2 recognise their own outputs and score them higher than equally rated outputs from others, and self-recognition correlates linearly with self-preference ([Panickssery et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)). And judges are weak at creative quality: the best off-the-shelf judge in LitBench, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, agreed with human story preferences 73% of the time, against 78% for trained reward models ([LitBench, EACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.362)), judges are worst calibrated where human annotators disagree ([Artificial Hivemind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954)), EQ-Bench's own documentation says it does not control for self-bias or positivity bias ([EQ-Bench](https://eqbench.com/about.html)), and rubric scores rank measurably less tense stories above the New Yorker. Industry practice draws the same line. Larian's writing director Adam Smith said no text generation touches Divinity's writing and rated text-generation tests "a 3/10 at best", after Swen Vincke had described using generative AI for ideas and placeholder text ([GamingBible](https://www.gamingbible.com/news/baldurs-gate-3-developer-larian-stance-ai-writing-036364-20260112)). An auditing agent should therefore strip authorship framing, list findings as counts against a fixed checklist before any praise, and establish "has tension" or "is original" through structural checks and probes, never by its own rating.

### Countermeasures with evidence change the process, not the wording

The countermeasures that have measured effects act on structure and process. Outline-first pipelines improved coherence: Re3's plan, draft, rerank and edit loop made plots rated coherent 14 percentage points more often than direct generation ([Re3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06774)), and DOC's more detailed outline with a controller added a further 22.5% plot coherence and 20.7% interestingness ([DOC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10077)). Writing explicit turning points into the outline raised the share of stories rated most suspenseful from 7.9% to about 46–48%, though GPT-4 produced a requested arc only 54% of the time ([Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13248v2)). Adapting a reference story's beat structure to the new idea before generating raised Claude Sonnet 4.6's late-stage miss rate from 0.139 to 0.339 and its tension retention from 16.7% to 35.5%, closing about half the gap to New Yorker fiction; EQ-Bench scores fell for Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.2 under the method but rose for Sonnet 4.6, further evidence that the rubric does not track tension ([Spoiler Alert](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09854v1)). Divergence can be forced and then checked. Verbalized sampling counters typicality bias, with its 1.6–2.1× diversity gain covered in the dialogue report, and the same "sample many and compare" logic turns "is this beat the default?" into a procedure: sample many continuations from the outline so far and see whether the planned beat is among the common ones. One such score correlated with human surprise ratings at Spearman 0.55 ([Echoes in AI](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00273v1)). Grounding in specific material displaces defaults. Fine-tuning a model on an author's complete works reversed expert readers' preference, which the authors attribute to removing detectable quirks such as cliché density ([Chakrabarty et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13939v4)); a prompting agent cannot fine-tune, but the mechanism supports feeding it the game's mechanics and verbs, setting research and a named reference work. Banned lists belong in the checker, not the prompt. Antislop found token banning unusable at around 2,000 patterns and noted that instructing models to avoid vocabulary has limited effect and may backfire ([Antislop](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15061)), and a preprint documents the "pink elephant" effect of naming a topic to avoid ([Castricato et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07896v2.pdf)). Whether banning one default yields the next (Elara to Lyra) has not been measured, which is why conformance to the setting's own naming rules is the stronger check. The table maps each documented failure to the check a skill can run.

| Failure | Evidence | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Climax and major setback front-loaded | Tian et al. | Record each turning point's position as a share of the beat map; the largest reversal sits in the final stretch |
| Peaks fully resolved | Spoiler Alert, 22% vs 54% retention | After each peak, a cost, loss, open question or changed relationship carries forward |
| One arc shape | Tian: man in a hole in over half of GPT-4 stories | Arc chosen deliberately; a tragic, bittersweet or falling candidate considered |
| Friendly cast | Nonaka & Perry | Cast map holds persistent adversarial ties; at least one survives the ending unreconciled |
| Festival or heart-to-heart resolution | Rettberg & Wigers; Wordcraft | The central conflict resolves through a costly action that changes world state |
| Default names, settings and titles | Hamilton & Mimno; Antislop; Rettberg & Wigers | Post-generation scan for Elias, Elara, Mara, Kael, lighthouse-keeper and craft-profession templates, "Whispering" and "Last" titles; names checked against the setting's naming spec |
| Template co-occurrence | Hamilton & Mimno; Rettberg & Wigers | Flag a quiet craft profession plus a small-town or coastal setting plus a "secret" plus heavy light imagery, not single tokens |
| Default beats | Echoes in AI; Spoiler Alert | Probe: sample 10–20 continuations per key beat and the ending; replace or justify planned beats that match the majority |
| Accepting the first premise | Verbalized sampling; Si et al. | At least five premises from varied inputs, deduplicated; the modal one chosen only with a stated reason |
| Stated theme | Dramatron; *Art or Artifice?* in the dialogue report | Scan for lesson statements ("learned that", "what truly matters", "power of friendship") |
| Example leakage | Wordcraft's "Sarah" | Placeholders or rotated, deliberately odd examples in the skill's own templates; scan output for them |
| Softened audit and self-certification | Sharma; Panickssery; LitBench | Blind, checklist-first audit; no "original" or "tense" verdicts from the model itself |

## A story skill should interrogate, structure and check, not invent

The findings above assemble into two ordered workflows, one for creating a story and one for auditing an existing game. Each stage exists because a documented failure happens when it is skipped. The tables are a synthesis to condense into the skill, and the grounding column points back to the sections above.

| Create stage | What the agent does | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Read the project | Mechanics and verbs, loop, genre, existing canon, VOICES.md, constraints (voice budget, localisation, platform) | Late narrative inherits a fixed story space; game-dialogue reads the same canon |
| 2. Intake | Owner's geewhiz, tonal references with one scene each and their not-wanted parts, pillars, protected moments, personal and researched particulars, inspirations and what they never did, borrowed traditions and reviewers | Pillars, Mom Test, Celeste and Papers, Please particulars, inspiration ledger |
| 3. Diverge | Owner sketches one or two rough alternatives; agent generates a wide pool from different generators (bisociation, imposed constraint, opposing viewpoint, owner's angle), cuts the familiar, deduplicates | Kelly, Koestler, jam constraints, Berg, verbalized sampling, Si et al. |
| 4. Test premises | One-sentence and logline failure tests, Story Spine per finalist, "what does the player do that moves this story?" | Ingermanson, Snyder, Adams, Kasavin |
| 5. Present and choose | Two or three genuinely different options with risks and gains, originality stated as a criterion, no authoritative self-ranking; lock pillars of the chosen one | Rietzschel, Berg, Deck Nine, Braintrust |
| 6. Build canon | Truth, tellings, cast with wants and stances, antagonist with front, world rules with costs, naming spec, tone contract, both gameplay contracts | Construction, cast and world, and tone sections |
| 7. Break the beat map | Snowflake disasters to chapter gates; lettered strands blended into play spaces; beats as player-facing world-state changes with IDs, preconditions, postconditions, carriers, fallbacks and gates; midpoint legibility; peak placement | Haywood, Doran & Parberry, Larian, completion data, Journey |
| 8. Check | Mechanical scans and structural checks (causal links, value turns, setup ledger, coincidence, promise ledger, turning-point positions, residue, cast signs, default probes), then regenerate flagged items without putting ban lists in the prompt | Model-failure table; Antislop |
| 9. Hand off | STORY.md with statuses, open questions and a changelog; protected elements; consultation flags; a playtest plan; no claims of originality or authenticity | Canon control, narrative playtesting |

| Audit stage | What the agent does | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Inventory story space | Cutscene slots, voice budget, channels, environments, what is recorded and locked | Narrative designer joining late |
| 2. Extract the as-built story | Normalise every unit to a row, build the graph, run reachability, flag, reveal-order and contradiction checks | Manning, graph validators, Anthem |
| 3. Separate built from intended | Design-doc beats with no implementing unit, and implemented units with no design mention | Design documents describe cut and unimplemented content |
| 4. Diagnose blind | Five Commandments per unit, Six Core Questions globally, construction and game checks, model-failure checks; findings counted before any praise, without authorship framing | Story Grid, Sharma |
| 5. Report as coverage | Logline of the as-built story, synopsis, graded grid, notes keyed to unit IDs, verdict of revise or rewrite | Script coverage |
| 6. Offer options | Two or three fixes per problem, each with trade-offs and the units, lines, voice and cinematics it touches; additive fixes first; spine replacement only on spine failure, priced | Destiny, Anthem, ME3, Hades II, Braintrust |
| 7. Protect and record | Owner's protected elements recorded first; a change-impact table (keep, re-motivate, rewrite, re-voice, cut) keyed to IDs for game-dialogue and production | Portal 2, Chandler, IGDA drift |
| 8. Plan the test | Retell, character perception, reveal checks, per-scene skip telemetry | Hendersen, Dankoff, Kreminski |

Two defaults deserve emphasis because the evidence for them is strongest. The agent should treat a beat's world-state metadata as part of the beat: a beat without preconditions, postconditions and a carrier cannot be checked for order, leaks, soft-locks or payoffs, and it cannot be handed to quest design. The agent should also never certify its own story as original, tense or authentic. Models judge their own creative work poorly and generously, the defaults they fall into are invisible to rubric judges, and the craft's decisive tests (whether players can retell it, whether the peak lands, whether the culture is represented fairly) happen in playtests and with people, not on the page.

## Conclusion

The central shift is in what counts as the unit of a game story. It is neither a scene nor a cutscene but a beat defined as a change in world state that some carrier delivers, with preconditions, postconditions, a fallback and a theme link. That one definition unifies advice that otherwise looks scattered. McKee's value turn, Doran and Parberry's preconditions, Larian's n+1, Sasko's delayed-but-visible consequences, Jenkins's redundant clues and Hades' eligibility rules are all checks on the same object. It also explains why STORY.md has to be a canon file with statuses and stable IDs rather than a narrative synopsis: game-dialogue needs to know what each speaker knows at each stage, game-quests needs beats it can translate into verbs without breaking causality, and an audit needs units it can diff when gameplay changes. A synopsis serves none of them.

The second finding is that the craft's own tools amount to a specification of the choices a language model will not make unprompted. Causal links counter the model's episodic drift. Late turning points and carried residue counter its early, total resolutions, adversarial ties its friendly casts, costed world rules its frictionless worlds, and owner-sourced particulars its lighthouse keepers. The evidence says these must be enforced by checks, probes and an owner's choices rather than requested in a prompt, because the defaults are trained in, are shared across vendors and are invisible to model judges. What remains uncertain is quantitative and game-specific. No study has measured model failures on game narratives, no public standard fixes beat spacing, segment length, cast size or reactivity budgets, and the folk-horror, creature-collector and roguelite guidance rests on a handful of documented games. A skill built on this report should expose those numbers as parameters, act as interrogator, structurer and checker rather than as an autonomous author, and end every delivery by naming what only the owner, a playtest or a consultant can settle.
